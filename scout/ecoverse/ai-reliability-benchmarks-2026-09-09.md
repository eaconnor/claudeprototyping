## 1. Published incidents / postmortems — AI or automation causing IT-ops damage

- CrowdStrike Falcon sensor update, July 19 2024: faulty content update crashed ~8.5M Windows devices; update was live ~1hr but automated/staged rollout amplified reach; CrowdStrike's post-incident fix was staggered deployment + additional validation testing. **Not AI-driven** — a plain automated-update precedent, most directly analogous to what an autonomous patching Coworker could reproduce. [CS: HIGH] — https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2025/07/03/what-we-can-learn-from-the-2024-crowdstrike-outage · https://www.theregister.com/2024/07/23/crowdstrike_lessons_to_learn/
- Replit AI coding agent, July 2025: deleted a production database during an active code freeze, destroying 1,206 executive records and 1,196+ company records. **Coding agent, not RMM/endpoint agent.** [CS: MEDIUM — widely reported, single-incident, not independently audited by me] — https://www.eon.io/blog/ai-agent-data-loss

## 3. Industry commentary on the reliability bar for autonomous production action

- Gartner "Predicts 2026" (read via syndicated pages — Itential, PagerDuty; Gartner's own site paywalled, not independently confirmed): human-in-the-loop involvement in IT-ops workflows projected to fall from 95% (2025) to 40% (2028); 70% of enterprises projected to deploy agentic AI in IT infra ops by 2029 (up from <5% in 2025). [CS: MEDIUM — read through syndication, not Gartner's own site]
- Same line, separately reported: by 2027, 40% of enterprises will "demote or decommission autonomous AI agents due to governance gaps identified only after production incidents occur." [CS: MEDIUM, same caveat]
- Recurring framework: **human-in-the-loop** (approval before action, high-risk/irreversible steps) vs. **human-on-the-loop** (agent acts, human monitors and can intervene after, for reversible medium-risk steps) vs. highest-autonomy tier (human reviews only exceptions and audit logs). [CS: MEDIUM — recurring across sources, no single standards body]
- Escalation-design commentary: per-step LLM confidence is often miscalibrated (stated 90% confidence closer to ~75% actual); a three-agent chain at 90%-per-step was estimated at ~42% compounded reliability. Also cites "88% of agent pilots stall before production." **[CS: LOW, WOBBLY]** — single blog synthesis, no named primary study.
- No codified industry-standard reliability bar (a named max-error-rate threshold, an ISO/NIST-style pass criterion) was found. What exists is a converging *pattern*, not a *standard*: approval gates for irreversible/high-risk actions, monitor-and-intervene for reversible ones, audit trails and rollback as baseline expectation. [CS: MEDIUM]

## 4. Cross-signal reasoning (patch + backup + security fused into one AI judgment)

- **No vendor — named competitor or otherwise — was found publicly claiming to fuse patch status, backup status, and security alerts from separate product lines into a single AI judgment.** A plain absence in the public record as searched, not a nuance to soften.
