### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- This bets on a functional job (audience-segmented sharing) nobody's shown is unmet, since the "job" being hired is inferred from an internal thesis rather than from what dissatisfied users of the dominant network are actually firing it for. [A]

### Service Blueprint -- Mandatory sign-up through mail/video/photos means the frontstage "choice" to join Circles is largely fictional, and the brief never addresses the backstage support cost of onboarding users who never intended to use a social product. [D][?]

### Self-Determination Theory -- Forced account creation via other products directly undermines autonomy at the exact moment the OKR needs users to feel they're choosing this, which is the core design contradiction the brief doesn't name. [A][D]

### Disruptive Innovation -- This reads as a sustaining move dressed as disruptive — bundling into entrenched products to borrow their distribution rather than attacking the incumbent from below with something genuinely simpler or cheaper — and Christensen's frame would say a late entrant with feature parity plus forced distribution has no low-end or new-market wedge. [A]

### Value Chain -- The insertion point is identity/social layer defense, not a new value creation node, so the "margin effect" is purely defensive (protect the ecosystem chokepoint) with no evidence it adds margin on its own. [?]

**Divergence:** JTBD and Disruptive Innovation both question whether there's a real unmet job/wedge, while Service Blueprint and SDT converge on the forced-integration mechanism itself as the design flaw — this is two separate objections (is there a problem worth solving vs. is the delivery mechanism corrosive to the people forced through it) that the brief conflates into one risk.
