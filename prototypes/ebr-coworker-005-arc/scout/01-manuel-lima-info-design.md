# Manuel Lima — documented visualization taxonomy (for findings/report screen)

Source pool note: [WOBBLY] — single author/curator. Treating Lima's taxonomy as the
vocabulary for "beautiful but legible" design is a narrow tradition; no second scholar
cross-checked in this pull.

## Primary works
- *Visual Complexity: Mapping Patterns of Information* — Manuel Lima, Princeton
  Architectural Press, 2011. [CS: HIGH]
  - ~15 named network-diagram types incl. arc diagrams, radial convergence/implosion
    layouts, sphere/globe layouts, flow diagrams, organic rhizome forms.
  - Ch. 3 contains a short section of design principles for network visualization
    (specific list not verified firsthand — flag before quoting principles directly).
  - https://www.eyemagazine.com/review/article/networks-map-a-complex-world
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Lima
  - https://archive.org/details/visualcomplexity0000lima

- VisualComplexity.com — Lima's own archive project, founded 2005. [CS: HIGH]
  - 770+ indexed projects, browsable by domain (biology, physics, network science,
    sociology, government/news data).
  - Useful as a reference library of *deployed* examples per diagram type, not just
    theory.

- *The Book of Trees: Visualizing Branches of Knowledge* — Manuel Lima, 2014. [CS: HIGH]
  - 200+ tree-diagram examples spanning ~800 years — medieval illuminated-manuscript
    hierarchies through contemporary design.
  - Directly relevant to nested/hierarchical category visualization (e.g. findings
    grouped by area → sub-finding → data point).
  - https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18269572-the-book-of-trees

- *The Book of Circles: Visualizing Spheres of Knowledge* — Manuel Lima, 2017. [CS: HIGH]
  - Explicit 21-type taxonomy of circular diagrams, grouped into 7 named categories:
    Rings & Spirals, Wheels & Pies, Grids & Graticules, Ebbs & Flows, Shapes &
    Boundaries, Maps & Blueprints, Nodes & Links.
  - https://www.amazon.com/Book-Circles-Visualizing-Spheres-Knowledge/dp/1616895284
  - https://papress.com/products/the-book-of-circles-visualizing-spheres-of-knowledge
  - https://medium.com/@mslima/the-book-of-circles-4b511a5bcffc

## Recurring thesis (not a single citable passage)
- "Beautiful AND legible" — Lima's repeated public framing (talks, incl. TED talk "A
  Visual History of Human Knowledge," 2015) that visualization should marry aesthetic
  form with functional clarity, arguing against decoration for its own sake. [CS: MEDIUM]
  — this is a pattern across multiple public talks, not one quotable source line.
