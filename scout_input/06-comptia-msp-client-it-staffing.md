# CompTIA data on MSP client-side IT staffing (adjacent, not identical, question)

**Source:** CompTIA Connect research/blog, referenced via "Trends in Managed Services 2022" and related CompTIA content. https://connect.comptia.org/content/research/trends-in-managed-services-2022

[CS: MEDIUM] — pulled via secondary web-search summarization of CompTIA material, not directly fetched from a single verifiable primary PDF page. Treat percentages as directional, not exact-cite-ready.

Key data point, but note the unit of analysis: this describes **MSP client organizations** (the businesses that hire an MSP), not the internal structure of the MSP itself. Different population than Beth's question, but relevant to the same underlying assumption — how often does an org have a technical-user/economic-buyer split at all.

- 53% of companies using managed services kept internal IT headcount unchanged after onboarding an MSP.
- 15% had no internal IT staff before the MSP relationship — i.e., no internal "technician" role existed to separate from the buyer at all.
- Only 6% eliminated in-house IT staff entirely after adopting MSP services.
- 63% of MSPs are structured as "hybrid" (mix of pure-play managed services + break-fix/project work) vs. 37% "pure play," per CompTIA 2022 data. [CS: MEDIUM]

**Why this is here, and why it's not a direct answer:** This measures the client side of the MSP relationship (does the client company have a dedicated IT lead separate from whoever uses the MSP's output), not the MSP's own internal org (does the MSP's technician who racks up hours differ from whoever signs the PO for the MSP's own tool stack — e.g., N-able, ConnectWise, Kaseya). Beth's question is about the latter. This dataset speaks to a structurally similar but distinct question and should not be conflated with it.
