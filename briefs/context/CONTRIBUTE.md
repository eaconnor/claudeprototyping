# Saw something? Add it here.

For marketing, Head Nerds, sales, CS, support — anyone with customer contact who notices a pattern. **You don't need to learn the tags, the gates, or open the ux.md files.** You bring what you saw; the research apparatus adds the rigor.

This is the "in" side of the loop: the ux.md files push open questions *out* to research, and field insight comes *in* here. Both meet at the datadump.

## How to add something (four things)

Drop a note — in the datadump, a message to Beth, or a file in `scout_input/` — with these four fields:

1. **What you saw / heard** — the observation, in your own words. A quote, a pattern, a competitor move, a recurring complaint.
2. **Where it came from** — which partner/client/call/thread/report, and when. (If you can't name the source, say so — that's fine, it just changes how much weight it carries.)
3. **How sure you are** — "saw it once," "hear it constantly," "a hunch," "hard data." No jargon needed; whoever files it translates that into a trust tag.
4. **Who to credit** — your name. Your *read* of what it means gets attributed to you (like `[Coder: NAME]`), kept as yours — not flattened into "fact."

That's it. Someone (the researcher, or the coach) routes it into the right ux.md section and applies the trust tag. Your job is the observation; the tagging is mechanics done downstream.

## Three things that keep it honest (please read)

- **Two people saying the same thing isn't always two sources.** If you heard it from a partner *you trained*, that's an echo, not corroboration — say who taught whom. (We once counted a teacher and her student as two sources; they weren't.)
- **Your interpretation is welcome and stays yours.** A Head Nerd's read of what a partner *means* is expert judgment, tracked as your view — not merged into the evidence. That's a feature.
- **Customer data has rules.** Only bring in what was *offered* — shared or published with the intent of being used. Get consent for quotes. Minimize: bring what the point needs, not everything you can reach. (GDPR; UK jurisdiction. If you'd not be comfortable with it public, it doesn't belong here.)

## What happens to it

It lands in the datadump, gets a trust tag and provenance, and — if it answers an open question in a ux.md's **Open questions → datadump** table — folds into the body and the question closes. If it opens a *new* question, that's also a win: unknowns are the next agenda, not failures.
