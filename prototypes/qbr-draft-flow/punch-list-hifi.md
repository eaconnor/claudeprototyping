**Original ask, for the record:** Beth, looking at the actual Technica Solutions template (screenshot, `Executive Business Review - Redacted.xlsx`): "draft review - organize around their template -- you so didn't do that. It's long and terrible - your version. do better." Fair — this is the single biggest structural miss in the build.

The real template is **8 tabs**, in this exact order: `1. Agenda` · `2. Current Climate` · `3. Risk Review` · `4. Employee Review` · `5. Hardware` · `6. Technical Roadmap` · `7. Referrals & Testimonials` · `Follow Up Summary`. The Agenda tab is the entry point: a client-info header (Name of client, Meeting contact, Date of EBR, Location, Last EBR, EBR Frequency, Client Advocate) plus the numbered section list plus the next 3 scheduled EBR dates — then each subsequent tab is its own scoped page.

What this build did instead: one long vertically-scrolling `views.draftReview` page — title block, security score, environment summary, findings register, consultative questions — with no tab/section navigation at all. It borrows *some* of the template's content (risk register ≈ tab 3, environment summary ≈ tab 2) but never adopts the template's actual information architecture, which is exactly what made it "long and terrible": everything competing for attention on one scroll instead of being organized into the 8 scoped views a reader actually expects.

**What "better" looks like:** Draft Review gets its own tab strip (mirroring the screenshot's colored tab bar) with at minimum Agenda / Current Climate / Risk Review / Employee Review / Hardware / Technical Roadmap / Referrals & Testimonials / Follow Up Summary as real, separately-rendered sections — not one page pretending to be all of them. Existing content (score panel + environment summary → Current Climate; findings register → Risk Review) gets redistributed into that structure rather than rebuilt from scratch. Employee Review, Hardware, Technical Roadmap, Referrals & Testimonials, and Follow Up Summary are net-new sections this build has never attempted (see also item 4 below — template richness deferral).

This is a real restructure, not a small patch — recorded as PRIME rather than built immediately, per "punch list prime."

**Design refinement, Beth: "finalize means the deck is ready to go to the meeting. it could be finalized in stages."** This resolves how the tab strip should carry readiness: not one global "Ready for room" pill gating the whole draft (current mechanism), but a per-tab finalize state — each of the 8 tabs (Agenda, Current Climate, Risk Review, Employee Review, Hardware, Technical Roadmap, Referrals & Testimonials, Follow Up Summary) can be locked/finalized independently as its owner clears it, while others stay open. "Ready for the meeting" becomes a derived state — true only once every tab is finalized — rather than a single switch. Risk Review (Jordan's register) finalizing separately from, say, Employee Review is the realistic case: different owners, different pace.
