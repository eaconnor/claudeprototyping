### Service Blueprint -- The frontstage "sign up once, use everywhere" convenience hid a backstage real-names identity-unification project that users never consented to, and the objection is that the brief gives zero backstage detail (support flows, appeals process for suspended pseudonymous accounts) needed to run this analysis honestly. ### Self-Determination Theory -- Mandatory sign-up is a direct autonomy violation by design, and it further eroded competence and relatedness for communities (YouTube commenters, non-legal-name users) forced into a network they didn't choose, which is the strongest objection this framework can raise against a "mandatory" brief on its face. [CS: HIGH — "mandatory" is stated in the brief itself]

### Disruptive Innovation -- This reads as a sustaining defensive move against Facebook rather than a disruptive one, and the objection is that bundling into existing high-usage products (mail, video, photo) to force adoption is incumbent behavior, not innovation — it defends Google's search/ads position rather than creating new value. [CS: MEDIUM]

### Value Chain -- Google inserted an identity layer across mail/video/photo to control the ad-targeting chokepoint and protect margin, but the objection is that coercing the chokepoint rather than earning it converts a margin play into a trust liability across the whole value chain. [CS: MEDIUM]

Jobs-to-Be-Done and Value Chain diverge on whether the mandatory-integration logic was sound: JTBD reads it as a category error (wrong job assumed), Value Chain reads it as a correct margin play executed coercively — that's a decision about what actually failed, not a gap in the brief.
