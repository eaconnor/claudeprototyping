# The ACP problem statement — three candidates, ranked by what actually holds

Not one answer — three altitudes, because the board's own Idea Chess baseline scored the
ambitious version at **~5%** and a smaller version at **~45–50%**, and a third reframe exists
that nobody has scored yet because it depends on two decisions only Beth/Meaghan/Nicole can make.
Presenting all three so the choice is visible, not made by omission.

---

## Candidate 1 — RECOMMENDED: the reframe ("agentic business operating system")

**Tension (Kolko):** MSPs are trying to prove they're becoming more valuable and less exposed as
a business, but every tool available to them — including AI — only proves they *did work*, not
that the work compounds into anything a client, an insurer, or a buyer would trust.

**Problem statement:** MSPs have no scalable way to prove they're maturing as a business, not
just staying secure. AI already generates a passable report — that bar dropped to zero the moment
"just ask Claude" became a viable starting point for any MSP `[CS: HIGH — SME call, Lewis Pope]`.
What no competitor can quickly copy is codified expert judgment (N-able's own Head Nerds)
delivered as a guided journey that *certifies* real operational maturity over time — compounding
into a liability position no one else can casually match.

**How Might We:** How might we turn a codified "Head Nerd" judgment into a guided journey that
certifies an MSP's maturity over time — building a moat a competitor can't replicate just by
wiring an LLM to the same data?

**Why this one, not a bigger ask:** the moat isn't the data (Lewis: "the data moat is short-lived
… trivial for them to do the same thing") and isn't the report (commoditized). It's four
compounding locks, each requiring the one before it: the Head Nerd corpus → a certification
engine that needs that corpus → insured liability transfer that needs the certified evidence
chain → owned distribution (already N-able's). A competitor has to beat all four, not one.

**Status: unscored as its own thesis.** Gated on two human decisions, not evidence:
1. Pivot to an advisory/business-OS motion (sell to owners on maturity + valuation) vs. stay a
   tools vendor selling features?
2. Buy the parity layer (control-plane plumbing, OEM/M&A) now, so build capacity goes to the
   moat — yes or no?

**Named refuting moves (kept, not hidden):** this is an org-capability bet — N-able would have to
become a business-coaching company, and the same techie-default that affects MSP owners likely
lives inside N-able too `[A]`. The insurance piece could *increase* N-able's liability exposure,
not reduce it — legally heavy, routed to counsel, not designed here. Zero MSP buyer validation
yet — n=3 internal experts + prior qual. **A hypothesis to field, not a plan to fund.**

## Candidate 2 — the narrow, tactical version (QBR/EBR alone)

**Tension:** MSPs are trying to prove their strategic value to the client every quarter, but the
evidence is scattered across 6+ vendor tools that don't speak to each other — so they either burn
hours hand-building the story or walk in with a raw data dump. `[CS: HIGH — Guidepoint]`

**Problem statement:** MSPs hand-assemble every client review from scratch, every time — hours of
senior time per client, no consistent evidence framework, no number they can defend if a client
or an incident pushes back.

**Why this one:** it's the version that actually clears the board's own bar — **~45–50% survival
"in some viable form"** — a real coworker business, likely partner-fronted, ~$5M scale, a feature
in the stack rather than a control plane. Smaller ambition, much higher odds.

**The risk this version doesn't solve:** it's exactly the shape a competitor can commoditize
fastest. The checkmate named across 3 of 5 adversarial voices in the board pass: *a competitor —
ConnectWise, Kaseya, or ScalePad — ships an AI QBR/report generator inside the tool MSPs already
trust with the data, before N-able's control plane earns the foothold to expand into higher-
autonomy, higher-margin use cases. The wedge dies as a feature; the platform never gets its
at-bat.* `[CS: VERIFIED — recurred across 3 of 5 adversarial voices]`

## Candidate 3 — as pitched to the board (kept for contrast, not recommended)

**Problem statement (as presented):** ACP is "the system IT runs on" — a governed control plane
for AI agents, $2,200/mo blended, $15M ARR by Year 3 at 20% attach, governance as the moat.

**Why it's here:** not to recommend it — to show what the other two are reacting against, and why
"bigger" isn't automatically "better." **Scored ~5% survival**, killed by three verified findings,
not speculation:
- **Governance is parity, not a moat** — Auditability scores ✔ for 8 of 10 competitors including
  N-able (slide 25, line-checked). Two adversarial voices claimed N-able was "behind 8 of 9
  competitors" — **that was a fabrication, caught and corrected in the same pass**; the honest
  finding is the opposite (table-stakes, not a deficit) — same conclusion the skeptics reached,
  reached honestly instead.
- **The pricing contradicts itself on adjacent slides** — slide 34 (board-ready): $1,500–2,500+
  tiers, $2,200 blended, 20% of 3,000 MSPs, $15M ARR. Slide 35 ("back of envelope," the deck's own
  more honest framing): $800–1,600, 250–500 deals, $5M topline. Two different pricing universes
  one slide apart. `[CS: VERIFIED — both figures appear verbatim]`
- **The earliest stall is DEFINE, not DEFEND:** the deck itself says pricing and build-vs-partner
  "are not decided today," while other slides present a priced model, an M&A target, and an
  in-flight build as settled. Unresolved on the page it was pitched from.

## What ties all three together

Every candidate agrees on one thing: **QBR/EBR is the wedge, not the destination.** Candidate 1
uses it as the entry point into a guided journey; Candidate 2 treats it as the whole business;
Candidate 3's failure mode is mistaking the wedge for a platform before the platform has earned
it. The `qbr-ebr.context.md` work already done this session is real evidence for *all three* —
it just answers a different question depending on which altitude gets chosen.

**This choice is not mine to make.** Recorded here as three named, evidenced options so the
decision is visible — not defaulted into by which one got built first.
