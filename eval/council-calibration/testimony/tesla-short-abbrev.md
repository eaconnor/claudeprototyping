### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- The $100k Roadster hires a social/emotional job (performance status for early adopters), and the objection is that success on that job supplies zero evidence the mass-market car's functional job (affordable daily transport) exists or is served the same way. [CS: HIGH]

### Service Blueprint -- The brief's frontstage promise ("electric car") hides a backstage the sentence never names — charging infrastructure, service network, battery supply — and the objection is that none of that backstage capacity built for a $100k low-volume car transfers cheaply to mass-market volume. [CS: MEDIUM]

### Self-Determination Theory -- Early buyers get real autonomy and competence (a car built for driver mastery/control), but the objection is that the "eventually fund mass-market" framing treats them instrumentally as capital for someone else's future car rather than as the market being served, eroding relatedness to the stated mission. [CS: LOW]

### Disruptive Innovation -- This is a sustaining move at the premium end for existing sports-car customers, not a disruptive attack from below, and the strongest objection is that Christensen's own theory would predict this sequence — climb-then-descend — doesn't reliably transfer disruptive capability downmarket, since low-end disruption is built on cost structure, not brand halo. [CS: MEDIUM]

### Value Chain -- The insertion point is final assembly plus battery IP, controlling a margin chokepoint at the luxury tier, and the objection is that the brief names no mechanism by which that margin or IP advantage survives the cost-curve collapse required to hit a mass-market price point. [CS: LOW]

> Disruptive Innovation and Jobs-to-Be-Done diverge on whether the Roadster-first sequence is coherent strategy — JTBD treats it as a legitimate emotional-job market in its own right, Disruptive Innovation treats the sequencing itself as theoretically backwards. This is a decision, not a gap.
