The AI-era failure modes

Three ways AI quietly wrecks a brief
before anyone notices.

Part i. fail

The AI finished it
before you thought it.

The tell: the brief is fluent on WHAT and HOW. Thin on WHO and WHY. The model filled the gaps with plausible language and you let it.

What it costs: six weeks of beautiful work pointed at a problem the AI invented for you.
Part ii. fail

The model picked
the framework.

The tell: the AI suggested POEMS or Jobs-to-Be-Done before you finished thinking. You used it because it was offered, not because it fit.

What it costs: a deliverable that looks rigorous and explains nothing — generated structure, not earned structure.
Part iii. fail

The AI cleaned
your voice out.

The tell: you pasted the draft into the model and it came back smoother, smarter, and not yours. Nobody can tell who wrote it. Including you, in six months.

What it costs: the work passes review and quietly fails to move anyone. Fluent ≠ true.

Part ii. Build the right thing · frameworks against drift

When a lens is thin, a framework
forces the model — and you — to do real work.

Frameworks aren't deliverables. They're forcing functions — and against AI they do specific work: they make you fill cells the model would have skimmed past. You read them back, and most of the time you throw them away.

Generative models converge on the statistically central answer. A framework yanks the work off-center.

Same back catalogue I've been teaching for fifteen years. The point isn't that they're new. The point is they still do the work the model won't.

Three things to take

If the brief is honest,
the rest of the work knows it.

i. Solve right

Audit the AI
before you ship.

Run the four lenses on every AI-assisted brief. The cheap fix isn't a smarter model — it's a human catching what the model glossed over, in all four directions, before anyone signs off.

ii. Build right

Frameworks are
forcing functions.

POEMS, 4As, 5Es. The model will offer one. Pick for fit, not because it was offered. Fill the cells yourself — the AI's job is to widen options, not to choose.

iii. Build right

Voice is the
seed.

Draft in pieces. Let the model give ingredients — bullets, ellipses, WOBBLY tags, named assumptions. You assemble. You decide what's true. You decide what's done.
