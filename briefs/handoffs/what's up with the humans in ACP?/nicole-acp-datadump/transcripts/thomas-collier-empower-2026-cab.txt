--- CONTRIBUTIONS BY THOMAS COLLIER ---

[04:15-06:19] The discussion shifted to expanding services into new lines of business, focusing
on business process integration and platform consolidation.
-A speaker (Jeff Young) noted they are engaging more with back-office functions like HR,
finance, and operations, moving beyond traditional IT.
-This shift involves replacing legacy systems (e.g., Great Plains) with modern financial
operations platforms (e.g., Dynamics F&O), leading to business transformation and platform
consolidation.
-Another participant (Thomas Collier) added that while their company doesn't do
applications, they see an opportunity in the integration layer, linking systems like HR and
finance.
-The goal is to create common, reusable business process automations (an "orchestration"
layer) for standard corporate functions (sales, marketing, finance, HR) to deliver services more
efficiently.

[13:29-16:35] The discussion focused on the challenge of managing "Shadow AI" and the need
to balance governance with innovation.
-Nicole Reineke posed the question: "How are you thinking about AI once your customers
are starting to run [it] in their environments?"
-The initial consensus (Jeff Young) was that the first step is to test and discover what AI is
being used, as ignoring it is not a viable option due to risks from unauthorized deployments
(e.g., "the owner's kid").
-A strategy mentioned is a "manager of managers" approach: creating a wrapper to gain
visibility and governance over AI sprawl.
-A key challenge is balancing long-term opportunities with quick wins. A common starting
point with clients is establishing a Center of Excellence for governance and then identifying
quick wins to demonstrate value to the C-suite.
-A central tension was identified: the need to avoid stunting innovation and creativity while
implementing controls to mitigate risk. As one speaker (Thomas Collier) put it, the conflicting
message can feel like, "they give me AI but don't use it."
-Security's role is seen as starting with visibility: knowing what is happening, who is
prompting, and which AI tools and clouds are in use.

--- FACILITATOR CONTEXT (Nicole Reineke) ---

[00:12-01:21] Nicole Reineke outlined the meeting agenda and requested a recording of the
session.
-Nicole stated she has a set of discussion questions, a presentation on the future of AI
("noun escalator"), and the "enamel AI tenants" which guide the organization's approach to AI
from an operational perspective.

[01:21-02:55] Participants discussed their envisioned roles as trusted advisors to clients
navigating the rapid evolution of AI over the next one to three years.
-Nicole Reineke reframed the initial question from "What AI should we build?" to "What do
you see as your future roles concerning your customers?"
-A speaker (Christian Kelly) noted that a three-year AI forecast is difficult due to the rapid
rate of innovation, but the short-to-medium term goal is to be in the conversation, guiding
customer journeys, and having a compelling story about enabling AI for the business.
-The primary aspiration is to become the "trusted advisor" clients turn to for navigating their
AI journey, even without having all the answers.
-A key concern is to avoid a scenario where external "AI consultants" are brought in,
dictating strategy to both the company and their clients.
-The speaker highlighted the uncertainty of whether "scaffolding tools" or the "core models"
themselves will become the dominant force in AI development.

[02:55-03:12] Nicole Reineke shared the company's strategic perspective on the future
interaction of AI models.
-In response to a point about the rapid evolution of AI models, Nicole stated the company's
approach is based on the assumption that different models will communicate with each other.
-This perspective is considered the "safest way" to approach future AI architecture.

[10:30-11:15] A participant confirmed their company has formalized its AI efforts by creating a
dedicated internal team.
-Echoing the uncertainty of the AI landscape, a speaker (Nicole Reineke0) stated their
company is working to help customers build AI solutions to streamline business operations.
-Key Decision: The company has appointed its own internal Head of AI to lead projects and
scale out a dedicated AI team.
-The speaker acknowledged it is difficult to know if they are early or late in forming this
dedicated team.

[11:38-13:17] The group discussed the abstraction of technology, comparing the flow of data
between AI systems to the historical flow of network packets.
-A speaker (Nicole Reineke2) proposed that future work will focus on enabling data to flow
between systems at a higher level of abstraction, similar to how networking evolved to just make
data flow without needing deep expertise in the underlying packets.
-The focus will be on orchestrating data flow to "encapsulate that job function in the
infrastructure," making the infrastructure itself a platform.
-Nicole Reineke agreed, referencing the business book "The E-Myth," which advocates for
creating a detailed operating manual for every job function. She stated this level of process
definition is essential for building effective AI.
