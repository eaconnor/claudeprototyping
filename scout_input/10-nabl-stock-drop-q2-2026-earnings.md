## Cause, per N-able's own management (earnings call, 2026-08-10)
Primary source: N-able Q2 2026 Earnings Call Transcript, The Motley Fool (published 2026-08-17) — https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/17/n-able-nabl-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/
Cross-referenced mirror: Yahoo Finance — https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/n-able-nabl-q2-2026-155931342.html

CEO John Pagliuca: "Our update reflects two primary factors: First, the impact from a transition in go-to-market leadership; and second, shifting dynamics in the evolving UEM and EDR markets." [CS: HIGH]

CFO Tim O'Brien: "The second quarter has our largest cohort of contract renewals and renewal rates in this cohort, primarily in UEM and EDR came in below expectations." Renewal rates slipped from high-80% range to mid-80% range during the quarter. [CS: HIGH]

On UEM competition — Pagliuca: "UEM, there is one player that is gaining more market share, I'd say, than the other couple." Attributed to that competitor's "focus on the mid-market vis-a-vis MSP," not fundamental product superiority. **Competitor not named as Microsoft in any excerpt obtained.** [CS: HIGH]

On EDR pricing pressure — Pagliuca: "we're seeing some pricing pressure... they might be going to get a SentinelOne type of service from a different type of provider." Amended SentinelOne agreement cited as providing "pricing protection for N-able" and expanded SKU availability. [CS: HIGH]

Positive counter-data point: "Our targeted displacement campaign increased migrations against a top competitor by 60%." [CS: HIGH]
