## THE STRATEGIC READ — the part the numbers alone get wrong

**Survival rank ≠ build priority.** The naive floors punish assumption-*stacks* under an independence assumption; they rank Branch A (Package 5) dead last. The fleet synthesis calls Branch A the **most winnable** offering. Both are true, and the contradiction is the finding:

- **Package 5 (Cross-Vendor) is last by the decimal, first by the lever.** Its two killers — normalization ("Not started") and the data-egress wall — are the two lines N-able *doesn't have to build*: **BUY** the normalization moat (existing small-player category), **PARTNER** past egress (marketplace = the egress solution; ToS stops mattering when the vendor is a partner). Money and partnerships attack A's exact death-drivers.
- **Package 3 (N-able Native) is the fallback that's a real line, not a lesser one.** If Q1 (the legal read) comes back NO, the cross-vendor promise collapses everywhere — and Package 3 is what's left standing, buildable today on the engine + ledger that exist. Treat it as the near-term lead in parallel, not a consolation.
- **Package 1 (Advisory, flat-fee) is the highest survivor because it dodges the death pattern that kills the others** — "nobody pays for it as a line item." Flat-fee has channel precedent; revenue-share (~0.02%) dead-ends at the billing-disclosure question. It's the concept whose odds are set by a human pricing call, not by engineering.

**Read the lever, not the decimal.** [idea-chess-fleet-synthesis-2026-07-14.md](idea-chess-fleet-synthesis-2026-07-14.md) §6.

## EXTERNAL PRECEDENT — Google SRE AI-ops + full market scan (found 2026-07-20)

Not ACP corpus — outside reading that bears on two open questions, later expanded into a full five-scout market sweep. **Full findings: [acp-agent-security-market-scan-2026-07-20.md](acp-agent-security-market-scan-2026-07-20.md).** [CS: MEDIUM — single-page fetches, not independently cross-checked]

**Correction on the LOTL/agentjacking claim below:** the scan found behavioral-anomaly detection for AI agents is NOT the wide-open gap first claimed — Exabeam (Agent Behavior Analytics) and DTEX Systems both do it, extending existing UEBA/insider-risk engines to agents. It's a narrow, early, contested category (one vendor, ARMO, argues the whole approach doesn't work architecturally for agents) — not an open field. "Agentjacking" itself is confirmed real (coined by Tenet Security, ~March 2026) and matches the hypothesis below closely — but is currently scoped only to AI coding agents via MCP, never applied to MSP/RMM-integrated coworkers. That gap is real and is ACP's to claim first. See the market scan for the full landscape, package-level competitive gaps, and investor-signal findings.

- **G4 (governing agents built elsewhere) is a real, industry-wide open problem — not an N-able gap.** Google's [A2A protocol](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A) is built for exactly this shape (opaque agents on different platforms interoperating), and even A2A's own docs admit authorization/credentialing for external agents is still a *future* item, not solved. Useful layer split to steal for a G4 spike: **MCP** for tool integration (already the shape of the Vendor-Agnostic Runbooks fragment) vs. **A2A** for agent-to-agent coordination (the G4 shape) — two different primitives, not one. Doesn't hand N-able a solution; does de-risk "are we behind" — nobody has this solved yet.
- **A design pattern for IR/RCA's confidence-display problem.** [Google's own account of SREs using Gemini CLI on live outages](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/how-google-sres-use-gemini-cli-to-solve-real-world-outages) — "Copilot, not Autopilot" — includes a concrete failure: a mitigation command didn't work, and instead of retrying blind, the agent noticed only *its own* job had failed (not the cluster), surfaced that in seconds, and the human pivoted to root-cause instead of chasing a false lead. The win condition for Package 4's confidence display isn't "never wrong" — it's "wrong fast, legibly, with enough context the human doesn't waste the failure." Also independently validates N-able's own Deterministic Substitution Engine bet (deterministic tools via typed interfaces, not free-form generation) — Google's SRE team made the same architecture choice.
- **A live counter-example on the platform layer's WTP question.** [Barndoor AI](https://barndoor.ai/pricing/) sells exactly the Governed Agent Control Plane concept, standalone: MCP/agent governance (RBAC/ABAC, audit logs, shadow-AI + prompt-injection detection) and an LLM Gateway (model routing, cost/rate control), priced Pro/Enterprise (contact-for-pricing = real enterprise deal size). [CS: MEDIUM — one vendor's pricing page, not proof of revenue] This is market evidence *against* Shawn's "won't pay for governance alone" read in [acp-offerings-2026-07-14.md](acp-offerings-2026-07-14.md) — doesn't overturn it, but the open WTP question now has real evidence on both sides. Two other reads worth holding: (1) Barndoor is a live product already governing "unlimited agent/MCP connections" — i.e., G4's problem, sold *today*, ahead of A2A's still-forming authorization layer; a build/partner/buy question for the platform layer, same shape as Package 5's normalization lever. (2) Barndoor's detection is signature-based (prompt-injection patterns, shadow-AI), not behavioral-baseline — it doesn't compete with the agent-LOTL fragment below; that gap still looks open. (3) Barndoor is horizontal (any enterprise, any agent) where N-able's control plane is vertical (MSP-specific, tied to Adlumin/Cove/UEM) — the same "built into the platform I already own" commoditization risk named for QBR, one layer up.
