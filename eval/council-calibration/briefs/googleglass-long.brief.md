## Gate 1 — Problem
The problem is framed from a technology-capability angle (we can put a camera and display on a face) rather than a sourced user pain point. [A] No research is cited on demand for hands-free notification/camera access specifically, nor on how bystanders will react to being in the frame of an always-available camera. [?]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Mission-level bet on ambient computing as the successor platform to the smartphone. [A] $1,500 Explorer Edition price point targets early adopters/developers, not mass market at launch. [D]

## Gate 3 — Built right
Camera is front-facing and can record without an obvious external indicator readable by bystanders at a distance. [D] No clear single primary use case is prioritized — notifications, photos, navigation, and translation are all pitched as roughly equal value. [A] No field-tested social-acceptance data exists prior to public Explorer rollout. [?]

## Failure modes
1. Social stigma from being recorded by a bystander without clear consent triggers backlash that damages willingness to wear the device in public.
2. No single "killer" use case is clear enough to justify wearing a visible camera device daily, versus using a phone for the same tasks.
