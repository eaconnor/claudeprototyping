### Jobs-to-Be-Done — sharp reframe, sourced from the wrong seat

The design question ("get a technical operator into a CFO's room feeling like a consultant") correctly separates functional job (produce a defensible register), emotional job (confidence), and social job (be read as strategic advisor, not "IT guy"). The two-register scripting (CFO gets ROI, owner gets strategy) is job segmentation done right — most tools in this category collapse both audiences into one report. The fired incumbent is named explicitly and concretely: report generators, including one with a body count ("the one that already died at Zomentum") — that's a stronger falsification of the competing hypothesis than most JTBD writeups bother with.

But the job itself is inferred from Stefanie, the trainer, not observed in the operator who would hire the tool. Stefanie's magic-wand list being "three parts confidence to one part time" is evidence about what *she* thinks operators need, filtered through a coach's model of the job — not evidence of what an average, non-elite operator would say if you asked them cold. JTBD's classic failure mode is designing to the expert's narrative of the job rather than the struggling customer's actual one; the brief names this gap itself (Q2 unfielded) rather than hiding it.

**Strongest objection:** the scaffold is explicitly designed to fade as competence builds — but JTBD would ask what job N-able itself is hiring this product to do commercially. A tool that successfully retires itself from the user's workflow retires its own telemetry and its own renewal case. The sketch doesn't reconcile the pedagogy goal (fade) with the business model implied by "first-party telemetry for every pull" (usage-dependent value capture).

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Good
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** the job framing and fired-incumbent identification are unusually precise for this stage; held below Strong Good because the job is currently sourced from the coach, not the player, and that's the exact seat JTBD warns against trusting alone.

### Service Blueprint — the confirm-gate is a bullet point, not a mechanism

Frontstage is the room: operator presenting, script in two registers, five rehearsed hard questions. Backstage is a live account state, a consequence-grammar register draft, red/yellow/green grading. That's a coherent frontstage/backstage split for an early sketch, and per the stage-appropriate rule, "the rehearsal/fading-scaffold interaction design doesn't exist yet" (Idea Chess's own build-stage death) is *expected* at this gate — noted, not scored down.

What *is* structural: the handoff where an AI-drafted register becomes a client-facing, liability-bearing document. The sketch names this itself ("everything drafted into the register inherits the liability weight") and the corpus-level finding says this same chokepoint sits under 4 of 6 sibling concepts — that's not a local unbuilt-backstage gap, that's a chokepoint that recurs across the whole portfolio, which is the definition of structural in this framework's own scoring rule. Compounding it: the confirm-gate between AI draft and operator-owned final language — the single mechanism that would make the liability handoff visible and defensible — is described as intent ("manual-first, her pedagogy") with no designed mechanic. R2's own framing of the open question ("seatbelt or nag?") is Service Blueprint's exact question, unanswered.

**Strongest objection:** a support process this brief does not address at all — who reviews the register before the operator walks into a liability-exposed room, and on what cadence — is not named anywhere in Sketch 1's text. It surfaces only in the appendix as R1, aimed at the whole portfolio, not scoped to this sketch specifically.

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Neutral
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** stage-appropriate unbuilt scaffolding (rehearsal UI, fade logic) doesn't count against it; the liability-inheritance handoff does, because it's named as structural at the corpus level, not local to this sketch's youth, and no visible confirm-gate mechanism yet exists to contain it.

### Self-Determination Theory — the best-aligned voice in the sketch, on paper

Autonomy: "manual-first, her pedagogy," and the explicit design goal that scaffolding fades as the operator's own notes take over — this is autonomy-supportive design stated as intent, which is rarer than it should be in this category. Competence: rehearsing "the five hardest questions before the room does" is direct, deliberate-practice-shaped skill-building, not just information delivery. Relatedness: the two-register scripting helps the operator read the room correctly for both audiences present, and the whole premise — "feeling like a consultant" — is explicitly a social/identity payoff, which SDT would flag as legitimate motivational fuel, not manipulation, if the confidence is earned rather than simulated.

The risk is named in the sketch's own death squares and it is exactly the SDT risk: "if the scaffold doesn't fade, we automate away the reps that build the skill." That's competence-substitution, not competence-support, and the difference between the two versions of this product is entirely in a mechanism that doesn't exist yet.

**Strongest objection:** intent to fade is a value statement, not a designed mechanic. SDT would ask for the actual decrement schedule — what specifically causes the scaffold to recede, and what happens to the design if the metric that makes the product look successful (continued high usage, telemetry volume) is in tension with the metric that makes the pedagogy succeed (declining reliance). Nothing in the sketch reconciles that tension.

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Good
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** intent and structure both point the right direction (autonomy, competence, relatedness all explicitly served), but the fade is currently a stated value, not an engineered decrement — held short of Strong Good until that mechanism exists.

### Disruptive Innovation — the classification itself is unresolved, and the brief says so

This reads as sustaining innovation aimed at the existing QBR/EBR practice — a richer product for operators who already run the meeting, competing against report generators (one already dead — Zomentum) and against Stefanie's own manual practice. Sustaining plays compete for existing demand with a better experience; that's what "confidence, not minutes" is.

But the brief itself flags the one data point that would flip this classification: R4, "the zero-QBR distribution — share of MSPs delivering none," explicitly unlocks "sustaining-vs-disruptive." If a meaningful share of MSPs deliver zero QBRs today, Practice Companion could be a disruptive play against non-consumption (serve people currently doing nothing) rather than a sustaining play against existing report-generator incumbents. This is not a gap in the underlying idea — it's an unresolved fork the idea's own authors flagged, and Disruptive Innovation as a framework cannot render a confident read without that number.

**Strongest objection:** the named "N-able superpower" — the Head Nerd enablement channel as distribution — answers *how it would reach the market*, not *what kind of market entry this is*. A distribution moat is not a disruption thesis. Citing it as the superpower risks the board reading "we have a channel" as "we have a disruption strategy," which this framework would reject as a category error.

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Neutral
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** not scored down for a flaw in the idea — scored down because the framework's central diagnostic question (sustaining vs. disruptive) is genuinely unanswered by the evidence given, and the brief itself names the exact research question (R4) that would resolve it.

### Value Chain — real margin insertion point, chokepoint N-able doesn't own

This sits at the churn-prevention / referral-generation node of the MSP's own value chain — arguably the highest-leverage node in a recurring-revenue business, since churn is the single largest margin destroyer in the MSP model and referrals are near-zero-CAC revenue. Positioned correctly as margin-protecting first (fewer churned accounts from bad QBRs), margin-expanding second (referrals from good ones). First-party full-stack telemetry is a genuine differentiator against point-tool report generators, who cannot see account state between meetings the way N-able can.

The chokepoint N-able does control: the Stefanie/enablement channel, human capital that competitors can't buy access to. The chokepoint N-able does not control, and does not name in its own "superpower" list: whoever determines liability exposure for AI-drafted, client-facing register content — MSP's own counsel, potentially the MSP's E&O insurer, a chokepoint entirely outside N-able's value chain and one the corpus-level finding says gates 4 of 6 sibling concepts.

**Strongest objection:** a value-chain map that names the moat you hold (telemetry, enablement channel) but omits the chokepoint you don't (liability ownership) is an incomplete map, not a complete one with a gap — the omission itself is the finding. The "N-able superpower" section reads as complete; it isn't.

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Good
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** clear, defensible margin-protecting insertion point with a real telemetry moat — held below Strong Good because the chokepoint that could stop the concept (liability ownership) sits outside N-able's control and isn't acknowledged in the sketch's own superpower framing.
