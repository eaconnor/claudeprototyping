## Survey Frequency Recommendations

### B2C Frequency Formula
[CS: VERIFIED — Qualtrics methodology]
- **Base frequency on customer interaction patterns:** multiply interaction frequency by 2
- **Example:** Monthly customer interactions → send surveys every two months
- **Hard ceiling:** "Sending more than two surveys per month feels excessive for most respondents"

### B2B Frequency
[CS: HIGH]
- **Best practice:** "Send your customer surveys quarterly"
- **Rationale:** B2B audiences interact less frequently with companies

### Transactional Surveys
[CS: HIGH]
- **Can be sent with every transaction** BUT must remain "ultra-low effort"
- **Question limit:** "Maximum of 4 questions" to prevent fatigue

### New Customer Relationship Window
[CS: MEDIUM — from Delighted source]
- **First 6 months:** Can request feedback monthly
- **After 6 months:** Reduce to "once a quarter or twice a year"

## Survey Length Guidelines

### Time Limits
[CS: HIGH — multiple sources confirm]
- **Critical threshold:** Surveys over 20 minutes cause many respondents to drop out
- **Ideal target:** 90 seconds with 3–5 items plus optional comments
- **High-frequency surveys:** Maximum 4 questions

### Question Types
[CS: HIGH]
- **Open-ended questions:** Minimize — they require more effort than multiple-choice
- **Display logic:** Use to show open-ended fields only to relevant respondents
- **Compound questions:** Avoid — ask one question per item

## Design Best Practices (Qualtrics 5-Point Framework)

[CS: VERIFIED — direct from source]

1. **Ask direct, unambiguous questions**
2. **Ask one question per item** (avoid compound questions)
3. **Provide mutually exclusive answer choices**
4. **Limit open text fields**
5. **Use consistent scale points and structures**

### Scale Consistency
[CS: HIGH]
- Maintain uniform scale structures throughout (avoid mixing 5-point and 7-point scales)
- Keep response order consistent across questions

## Segmentation and Personalization

### Targeting Strategy
[CS: HIGH]
- "Segment your audience and personalize surveys based on those segments"
- **Example:** Ask about a specific product they purchased rather than broad questions
- **Impact:** "Shown to increase response rates and reduce mid-survey drop-off"

### Frequency Caps
[CS: MEDIUM — from HubSpot/Delighted sources]
- **Recommended cap:** One invite per 14 days per person
- **Exclusions:** Users with open tickets or recent responses
- Exclude those who recently interacted with support

## What Causes Survey Fatigue

[CS: HIGH — consensus across sources]

1. Too many questions
2. Extensive follow-up questions
3. Repetitive questions
4. Too many open-ended questions
5. Poor format/design
6. Lack of specific goal/focus
7. Overly complex language or jargon

## Application to Monthly Candy Survey

**For monthly recurring survey targeting small office (10-50 people):**

[ASSUMPTION] — These are guidelines, not tested specifically for monthly candy preference tracking in small populations:

- **Frequency:** Monthly may be acceptable IF survey is 90 seconds or less (3-5 questions max)
- **Alternative:** Consider bi-monthly (every 2 months) if including qualitative questions
- **Scale consistency:** Critical for tracking preference changes over time
- **Segmentation:** May not apply to very small populations (everyone gets same survey)

[WOBBLY] — No source specifically addresses "monthly preference tracking in populations under 50." Guidelines are extrapolated from B2C/B2B customer feedback contexts.
