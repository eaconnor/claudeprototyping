## Per-bet earliest fall

- **BET-A · Visible 9-step run-trace on Dashboard — falls at Launch/Adopt. [A: med].** Build survives clean — a collapsible stepper is just UI, ships regardless of content. But the trace's whole selling point is showing the judgment process open, and the two steps most likely to get expanded first — **Continuity/delta** and **Judge the 8 tabs** — are exactly the two steps the brief itself marks `⟨VERIFY: does David actually open on the delta?⟩` and `⟨VERIFY: David's exact 8 tabs and gap-logic⟩` (Section B, steps 2–3). A viewer who opens the trace to see the "black box" opened hits a placeholder in the two windows that matter most. The brief also admits the step count itself is unresolved: "8 visible stepper stops, not 9... flagged, not resolved either way" (Section B, step 5). The transparency mechanic is real; two of its eight panes are still boarded up.

- **BET-B · Honesty badge as the fix for the in-memory stub — falls at Launch/Adopt if incomplete; survives to Defend if all three sites ship clean. [A: med].** The brief predicts its own failure mode in writing: "Honesty badge ships at 1–2 of 3 required sites, most likely dropped from the Runbooks screen" (Failure Modes §2) — and the Runbooks screen is also the single most crowded build target (all 9 Figma states plus two new fixes: version-status pill, locked-by-name+TTL). The badge is competing for build-agent attention against the most engineering-dense screen in the spec. Second shot, independent of completeness: the disclosure is hover-only ("dashed-border chip... hover tooltip") on a two-pane list a decision-maker is more likely to skim than probe — for anyone who doesn't hover, "caveat present" and "caveat read" are not the same thing. If it ships whole and gets hovered, it defends the Zero-Vector honesty mandate (§10b) cheaply and durably; if either condition fails, it falls exactly where it was built to prevent — someone believing an in-memory stub is a real relationship.

- **BET-C · Apex Bridge as the visual system — survives Build; falls at Launch/Adopt on a labeling mismatch. [A: low].** The amendment (§5) rejects Will's Figma skin on the grounds that "his file's own AILERON purple reskin is a rough exploration, not the real system." But the brief's own Visual System section, in the same breath it endorses Apex Bridge, cites `build.md`'s description of that system as "Bethproto's own approximation of n-able's real Apex 2.0 design system" — the identical disqualifying property just used to reject option one. The choice between two non-canonical stand-ins is a practical one (tokens exist, already documented), not the "which one is real" argument the amendment's own language implies. Low band because the immediate audience (Beth, David/Vanessa sign-off) isn't demoing pixel fidelity to N-able's actual design org — the mismatch only bites if this artifact is later shown to someone Apex-literate who takes "Apex Bridge" at face value.

- **BET-D · role-play-simulator.html linked-but-not-MVP — survives Build; falls at Launch/Adopt on link/data drift. [A: med].** The brief names its own failure mode: "the simulator's link is visually labeled, not just differently routed" is the entire mitigation for "the 'not MVP' label gets lost and it reads as core flow" (Failure Modes §3) — a label is one CSS refactor away from disappearing, and nothing enforces it structurally. Sharper shot: role-play-simulator.html is the *source* of "the real 9-step operator vocabulary" that BET-A's dashboard trace is trying to make credible, yet its `FINDINGS`/`AREAS` data is explicitly *not* part of the reconciliation work (File layout: reconciliation covers `ebr-app.html`/`build-ebr.html`/`role-play-simulator.html`'s "three incompatible shapes," but the simulator itself stays a separate, unreconciled file per §7/Amendment 1). A curious viewer who clicks through to check the vocabulary's origin can land on a divergent dataset from the one the main flow now shows.

- **BET-E · Restoring 004's primitives grid + intent-tier table — falls at Launch/Adopt. [A: med].** The justification in both plan (§2) and brief (Section F) is lineage-continuity — "content confirmed present there, absent everywhere after" [D] — not a named present-day technician problem. That is precisely the pattern CLAUDE.md §3's Anchor requirement forbids without a check: "a taxonomy is not a deliverable... if Beth's fragment doesn't name the problem, ask before generating frameworks, categories, or domain typology." Nothing in either document names what setup failure the grid fixes for a technician moving through an already-7-step wizard; it stacks a second live invention on top (deriving the undefined "EBR Review primitive" slot from Will's Figma vocabulary) onto a restoration whose own value case was never made past "it used to exist."

- **BET-F · Fresh vision-framing copy for landing.html — falls at Launch/Adopt on an unfalsifiable claim. [A: low, on "lands better" specifically].** Gate 3's verification list only checks presence — "confirm landing.html copy carries the resilience/£300k-story framing" — never persuasion. No audience is named, no reviewer, no comparison test. The bet as posed ("will land better than the current line") cannot be adjudicated by anything this brief asks for; it can only ever confirm the framing exists. It's also first-draft generation with no Beth text to edit against — Amendment 2 confirms "no earlier generation wrote finished marketing copy" — which is the one mode CLAUDE.md §1 names directly: "Never generate first... Her voice is the seed. You are the compost." The two named ideas (Maister thesis, £300k narrative) will ship regardless of the band; whether they land is a question this brief structurally cannot answer for itself.

- **BET-G · Grounding tightly in unfinished backend reality vs a faster, less-literal mockup — survives to Defend. [A: med-high].** The brief indicts its own rigor in its own words: "by the strict Gate-1 rule... this brief is not fully ready" (Evidence-ratio flag), and the critic's own spot-check of the math found "2 of 6 had no locatable inline tag." Most viewers of the rendered HTML will never see the 9-field schema or the `⟨VERIFY⟩` tags — that rigor lives in the brief and SOURCES.md, not the pixels — so on a per-screen basis this bet resembles the ACP-build precedent's Value Chain shot (BET6/Porter, `idea-chess-build.md`): investing at the least-visible link. But unlike that precedent, the payoff here is structural rather than cosmetic: a code-cited schema fact, a named in-memory-stub gap, and a documented honesty badge are reusable assets for every future EBR session — they end the "does Coworker have a runbook_id" argument permanently rather than requiring re-grounding each time. That compounding property is what survives to Defend; the individual screen it's grounding may still be judged thin on its own.

- **BET-H · FINDINGS/AREAS reconciliation as a hard pre-build gate — falls at Build. Three files have already drifted into incompatible shapes once with no gate in place; the only mechanism preventing a repeat is a documentation instruction inside the plan/brief, not anything enforced in the build sequence itself. **Update, post-Build-Pass-1 (2026-09-01):** this bet held — Pass 1's own report confirms the reconciliation was done first, before any new render function, with the richer `role-play-simulator.html` fields (`translate.cfo/owner`, `consequence`, `liability`) preserved rather than dropped. Flagging the win explicitly since this file predicted the miss.

- **BET-I · Single unified SPA (extend `ebr-app.html` directly) as the file-layout fix — survives Build; falls at Defend. The fix doubles down on the same single-file shape with substantially more content folded in — Setup merge, capstone diff, 9-step run-cycle stepper, two-pane Runbooks screen, three honesty-badge sites, primitives grid — with no maintainability provision named for the resulting file beyond "reconcile the constants" (BET-H).
