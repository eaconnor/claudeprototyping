MEAL tier — five voices, abbreviated pass.

### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- The functional job is "find the actual answer fast," which PageRank hires by treating links as votes, but the brief never checks whether users experience the emotional/social job of trusting a "portal" (email, news, one-stop) enough to fire it for a bare results page. [D]/[A]

### Service Blueprint -- The frontstage promise ("better search") has no backstage built to sustain it — no ad product, no enterprise sales, no distribution deal — so the blueprint's backstage is a two-person team improvising infrastructure the customer never sees is missing. [D]

### Self-Determination Theory -- A pure-relevance, no-manipulation results page raises user autonomy and competence by removing gameable spam, but the brief supplies zero evidence users will feel that autonomy gain is worth losing the relatedness of a bundled portal experience. [?]

### Disruptive Innovation -- This is classically disruptive — a simpler, "worse-monetized" product attacking incumbents' paid-placement business model from below — but the brief's strongest objection to itself is that a better free tool with no moat is trivially copyable by the incumbents it threatens. [D]/[?]

### Value Chain -- PageRank inserts at the discovery chokepoint of the customer's information-seeking chain, which is high-leverage, but the brief cannot show margin effect at all since no revenue model exists, meaning the "value chain" bet is entirely unpriced. [?]

> Disruptive Innovation and Value Chain diverge on urgency: Disruptive Innovation treats the lack of a moat as the near-term threat (incumbents copy it), while Value Chain treats the lack of a monetization model as the deeper threat (there's no way to capture the value even if the moat holds) — this is a sequencing decision, not a gap.
