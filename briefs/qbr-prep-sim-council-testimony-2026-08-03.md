### Self-Determination Theory — autonomy and competence are designed for; relatedness is silent

Autonomy: the confirm-gate is the load-bearing interaction and it's built to preserve human control on purpose — "auto-draft, human-clears... a passive warning is a fail" (F3, S4). The judge queue's empty fields ("business consequence," "roadmap priority") force the human to author the judgment rather than accept an AI-generated one — this is a deliberate autonomy-preserving design choice, not an accident of scope.

Competence: the 60/40 content-audit test and the cognitive walkthrough (G3-9/G3-11) are themselves competence checks on the tool, and the judge-queue's demand for the operator to fill consequence/priority herself protects her sense of earned expertise rather than pre-empting it.

Relatedness: absent by omission, not undermined. Nothing in the brief connects the operator to a broader community of practice — Stefanie's 15-tab template and magic-wand asks are mined as design grammar (AMENDMENT 2b), not represented as a living relationship the operator is inducted into or measured against peers within. No element of the artifact tells the operator "you're doing this the way good delivery leads do it" beyond the copy itself.

AMENDMENT 2's batch-run premise (S6, all 8 pulls as one Coworker run) is where the real SDT tension lives: automating pulls the operator never evidenced doing (G1-6) can't erode competence she never had — but the brief's justification for that read requires trusting G1-6/G1-7 as generalizable from n=1, which is exactly the thing not yet triangulated. The single mid-run approval gate is the compensating mechanism, but it covers one PII-sensitive pull, not the batch.

**Strongest objection:** the readiness score (V14) is a classic autonomy-eroding device if it ever graduates past the Bradley rail — it would shift the felt locus of "am I ready to defend this" from self-judgment to an externally-scored bar. The brief defuses this correctly for now (quarantined to internal rail, labeled "not an evidenced metric") but names no rule preventing it from migrating onto page 2/3 in a future iteration.

**Verdict [this lens only]:** Good
**Why this strength, not one notch over:** Autonomy and competence are actively and correctly protected at the two interactions that matter (confirm-gate, judge queue); relatedness is simply unaddressed rather than damaged, and the readiness-score risk is named and contained — none of that clears the bar to Strong Good because relatedness isn't even attempted.
