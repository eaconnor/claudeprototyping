## Core Definition

[CS: VERIFIED]
Longitudinal surveys collect data from the **same participants** at multiple time points to track changes, trends, and developments over time.

**Key distinction from cross-sectional:** Gathering data from a different sample at each time point could cause sample bias in results [CS: HIGH — SurveyMonkey].

## Question Consistency — CRITICAL

[CS: VERIFIED — multiple sources confirm]

**Primary principle:** "It's better to keep the questions identical for all surveys"

**Rationale:** "Research has shown that changing the way a question is asked can result in substantially different answers, even from the same people" [CS: HIGH — SurveyMonkey]

**Tracking accuracy:** "To examine trends with the most accuracy, you should try your best to ask the same questions in every survey you repeat" [CS: VERIFIED]

### When Questions Must Change
[CS: MEDIUM — implied from best practices]
If market conditions or research objectives shift significantly, changes may be necessary BUT must be documented and analyzed for impact on trend comparability.

## Study Design Requirements

### Establish Clear Objectives
[CS: HIGH — InnovateMR]
- Align research questions with business priorities
- Address "potential sources of bias or confounding" through appropriate sample sizes and data collection methods

### Maintain Data Quality
[CS: HIGH]
Use "validated measures, standardized instruments, and quality control procedures" to minimize errors and ensure measurement reliability across time points.

## Participant Retention Strategies

[CS: HIGH — InnovateMR framework]

Implement retention strategies including:
1. **Regular communication with participants**
2. **Provide incentives for participation**
3. **Address concerns or barriers**

**Rationale:** These efforts directly combat attrition bias that threatens longitudinal validity.

### Attrition Bias Risk
[CS: HIGH]
Participant dropout over time creates bias if those who leave differ systematically from those who remain. Retention strategies are not optional—they protect data integrity.

## Analytical Approaches for Longitudinal Data

[CS: HIGH — specialized statistical methods required]

**Standard methods include:**
- Growth curve analysis
- Hierarchical linear modeling
- Structural equation modeling

**Purpose:** "Explore patterns and relationships unfolding over time"

[CS: MEDIUM] — For simple preference tracking (like candy choices), simpler descriptive statistics and frequency comparisons may suffice, but trend analysis requires attention to individual-level change vs. group-level averages.

## Data Management Protocols

[CS: HIGH — InnovateMR]

Establish robust protocols for:
- Security
- Confidentiality
- Accessibility of longitudinal data over time
- Proper storage and backup procedures
- Protection against loss or corruption

**Special consideration for small populations:** [ASSUMPTION] In office settings with 10-50 people, confidentiality becomes MORE critical due to identifiability risk.

## Practical Application

[CS: HIGH]
"Translate the insights into actionable recommendations and strategic initiatives" by interpreting findings within market dynamics and consumer behavior contexts.

## When to Use Longitudinal vs. Cross-Sectional

[CS: HIGH — SurveyMonkey guidance]

**Use longitudinal when:**
- Tracking how individual preferences/behaviors change
- Understanding development or progression patterns
- Measuring impact of specific interventions over time

**Use cross-sectional when:**
- One-time snapshot is sufficient
- Population turnover is high (participants won't remain available)
- Research timeline is short

## Application to Monthly Candy Survey

**For tracking Edinburgh office candy preferences:**

[CS: HIGH — direct application of methodology]
- **Must survey same individuals** each month to track preference changes
- **Keep questions identical** across all monthly waves
- **Document any changes** to questions or options (e.g., adding new candy bar)
- **Track attrition** — if participants drop out, understand why
- **Protect confidentiality** — small office means responses may be identifiable

[WOBBLY] — No specific guidance found for "monthly food preference tracking in workplace settings." Principles are adapted from general longitudinal methodology.

**Tension:** Monthly frequency (from fatigue literature: keep very short) vs. longitudinal requirement (keep questions consistent, which limits optimization opportunities if early questions prove problematic).
