# MSPCentric.io — "How MSPs Actually Make Vendor Buying Decisions" (opinion piece, no cited data)

**Source:** https://www.mspcentric.io/blog/post/how-msps-actually-make-vendor-buying-decisions-and-what-it-means-for-your-sales-motion

[CS: LOW] — this is a sales/marketing consultancy's blog post targeted at vendors selling INTO the MSP channel. No survey, no sample, no methodology. Pure practitioner opinion dressed as market analysis. [FABRICATION RISK if treated as data] — do not cite this as evidence of a percentage or prevalence claim; it makes none.

## What it claims (unsourced, author's professional observation only)
- **Small MSPs (under 10 staff):** "the owner makes the call — but they're influenced by what their technical lead thinks about the integration and what their peers are saying at the user group or in the community forum." Note: this already implies the owner is often close to, if not identical with, the technical decision-maker in the smallest shops — which cuts *against* a clean two-party split, not for it.
- **Mid-market MSPs (10–50 staff):** decision "typically involves the owner or CEO, the operations lead, and whoever manages billing or the PSA — each evaluating the product from a different angle."
- General claim: "Most vendors assume MSP buying decisions are made by the owner or the technical lead, but the reality is messier, slower, more collaborative, and more peer-influenced than traditional B2B SaaS sales motions account for."
- Explicit claim that technical user and economic buyer are usually different people — but this is asserted, not measured, and the same piece simultaneously describes small-MSP owners as embedded in/adjacent to the technical evaluation, which is an internal tension in the source's own argument.

## Why it's included
This is the closest thing to "named industry commentary on this specific question" that surfaced. It is opinion, not evidence, and it is self-contradicting on the exact point Beth is pressing (owner-as-technical-lead overlap in small shops vs. a stated general claim that buyer/user are usually different). Use only as a pointer to where informed people are already uncertain — not as a citation for the brief.
