## Gate 1 — Problem
Demand for home video recording and time-shifted viewing is real and well-documented. [D] Whether a 1-hour maximum cassette length meets the demand — full films and sports broadcasts typically run longer — is not separately tested. [?]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Bet: being first to market with a proprietary, tightly-controlled format secures the standard before a competitor can establish an alternative. [A] Strategy is to license the format slowly and selectively rather than open it broadly to other manufacturers immediately. [A]

## Gate 3 — Built right
Cassette length is capped at 1 hour at launch, which does not cover a full feature film or most sports broadcasts without a mid-recording tape change. [D] A rival format is expected to enter the market shortly after, with longer recording time and a more open licensing approach reportedly under consideration by that rival. [D]

## Failure modes
1. The 1-hour recording length is a real functional gap against the primary use cases (film, sports) consumers want to record, not a minor limitation.
2. A closed/slow licensing strategy limits the range and price competition of available hardware compared to a rival pursuing open licensing, reducing retail shelf presence and consumer choice.
