What Jeff Found Useful About Ask N‑zo
1. It gives helpful, reasonable answers to exploratory questions
Jeff consistently framed N‑zo as useful for curiosity-driven questions, even when he was not yet using it for formal troubleshooting.
He described asking things like “why is this machine running slow?” and said it gave “a couple of good answers.”
He emphasized that none of his questions felt wasted or wrong, noting that the answers were “cool” and informative even when he was just experimenting.
Takeaway: Jeff values N‑zo as a low‑risk, exploratory assistant that helps him think through problems, not just execute predefined workflows.
2. It’s faster than his local LLM and faster than expected
Speed stood out as a clear positive, especially when compared to tools he already runs internally.
Jeff said N‑zo was “not as fast as the big cloud LLMs,” but was “way faster than the one we run” locally.
He explicitly said it was “faster than I think I probably thought it might be.”
Takeaway: Even without peak performance, N‑zo cleared Jeff’s personal speed bar and exceeded expectations relative to in‑house tooling.
3. It reliably points him to where to find data
While Jeff wants N‑zo to eventually deliver results directly, he still found value in how it guides him today.
When asking about things like disk usage, he said N‑zo “has no problem telling me how to go find that data.”
He described the output as “real, usable data” once the correct host name was provided.
Takeaway: Even in its current state, Jeff sees N‑zo as a dependable guide for navigating data and diagnostics.
4. It works well when the input is correct
Jeff was clear that when N‑zo has the right context, it performs well.
After correcting host name issues, he said it “did great” and “gave me real, usable data.”
He explicitly confirmed that the “why is asset running slowly” question worked well for him.
Takeaway: Accuracy and usefulness are there. The main friction is context capture, not answer quality.
5. He would absolutely adopt it if generally available
Despite limited usage so far, Jeff was unequivocal about future adoption.
He said “100%” when asked if he would adopt Ask N‑zo at GA.
He added that even limited interaction was “already cool enough” and made him want to use it more.
Takeaway: Jeff sees clear value today and strong potential as capabilities mature.
6. He appreciates the direction and ongoing innovation
Jeff expressed appreciation not just for the tool, but for the effort and roadmap behind it.
He said he “really does appreciate the work that’s gone into this” and likes seeing N‑able invest in new capabilities.
He explicitly asked for more information and said “please continue to throw it at me.”
Takeaway: N‑zo builds goodwill and credibility. Jeff sees it as evidence of meaningful innovation, not a gimmick. 
 
What Could Be Improved (with Jeff’s Quotes)
1. Lack of contextual awareness of what he’s currently viewing
This was Jeff’s strongest and most repeated improvement request.
He expected N‑zo to understand the machine or asset already open on screen:
“I started thinking that it did know that and I would ask it… it’s like garbage answer. And I’m like, oh, it doesn’t know that I’m talking about this computer.”
He had to manually re‑enter hostnames, which caused friction:
“I would have to give it that host name, which was cool till I like mistyped the host name a couple of times.”
His ideal behavior was clear:
“It would be cool if it was contextually aware of what you had in the flyout view.”
Improvement opportunity: Automatically infer context from the active asset, flyout, or detailed view.
2. It explains how to get data, but does not deliver the result
Jeff wants N‑zo to move from guidance to execution.
On questions like disk usage, he said:
“It has no problem telling me how to go find that data. But it doesn’t… I want it to be able to then give me the report instead of like here’s how you go get the thing.”
He summarized the desired outcome simply:
“I want it to be able to say like, and here it is.”
Improvement opportunity: Return direct answers, reports, or summaries, not just instructions.
3. Discoverability and placement limit usage
Jeff admitted that where N‑zo lives in the UI affects how often he uses it.
He is rarely in the current view where N‑zo appears:
“I’m not in that view. So I’m not used to that view existing.”
Even though he likes the new view, habit wins:
“I like the new view a lot. I just am not… I’m just never in it.”
Improvement opportunity: Make N‑zo accessible globally, not tied to specific screens.
4. Full‑screen experience feels constrained (possible bug)
Jeff appreciated full‑screen mode but found it incomplete.
He said full screen didn’t actually give him more working space:
“My only nitpick is it’d be cool if when you put it in full screen if it actually went bigger… it just sticks it in a column in the middle of the screen.”
He believed this might be unintended behavior:
“That really solved that whole thing for me… never mind. It doesn’t solve my problem.”
Improvement opportunity: True responsive full‑screen layout with wider text and content area.
5. Reporting issues and feedback paths are easy to miss
Jeff didn’t realize how to report UI problems.
He said:
“I didn’t think about reporting an issue with the size because I was like, oh, that must be intentional.”
He acknowledged the info was there, but not obvious enough:
“This is probably because I skipped over that part in the welcome e‑mail.”
Improvement opportunity: In‑product reminders or lightweight prompts for reporting bugs and feedback.
6. Host name ambiguity across tenants
Jeff raised a practical edge case that could become a broader problem.
He noted:
“It’s not uncommon for us to have the same machine name in more than one tenant.”
Improvement opportunity: Disambiguation when identical hostnames exist across tenants.
