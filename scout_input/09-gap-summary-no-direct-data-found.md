**No source — vendor-sponsored, trade-press, or independent — asks the specific question: "at your MSP, is the person who technically uses [tool category] the same person who approved paying for it?"**

What exists instead, ranked by proximity to the actual question:

2. Kaseya's own respondent-role taxonomy (43% executive / 57% "technician," where "technician" bundles IT managers, project managers, and sysadmins together) — demonstrates that even the vendor asking these questions doesn't cleanly separate "hands-on user" from "decision-adjacent role."
3. One uncited vendor-consultancy blog post asserting a buying-committee model for MSPs, internally inconsistent on this exact point.
4. CompTIA data on MSP *client* IT staffing — answers a structurally similar but different question about the client side of the relationship, not the MSP's own internal purchasing structure.
5. General B2B sales doctrine that role consolidation is more common in smaller orgs — not MSP-specific, not quantified.

**Bottom line for the brief:** the internal brief's kill-criterion assumption ("these are usually different people") is stated with more confidence than any available data supports. The counter-claim Beth is making ("most MSP technicians are skilled business workers, the split may be overdrawn") is *also* not supported by hard data — it's equally an inference. Neither side of this argument currently has a number. The honest move is to name the assumption as untested on both sides, not to resolve it with a fabricated stat in either direction.
