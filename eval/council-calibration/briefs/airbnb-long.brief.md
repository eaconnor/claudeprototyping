## Gate 1 — Problem
Founders observed hotel scarcity/price spikes during a local conference and rented air mattresses in their own apartment to cover rent, and strangers paid for it. [R, n=1 — not broader research] Whether this generalizes beyond conference-week scarcity to everyday travel is unvalidated. [?]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Bet: idle housing supply (spare rooms, empty apartments) is a viable substitute for hotel capacity broadly, not just during scarcity spikes. [A] No data yet on whether travelers will choose a stranger's home over a hotel when hotels aren't sold out. [?]

## Gate 3 — Built right
No trust/safety mechanism designed yet (reviews, verification, insurance). [?] Legal status of short-term home rental is unclear in most cities and untested. [?] No liability model if a stay goes wrong. [?]

## Failure modes
1. Stranger-danger trust barrier caps adoption to a small early-adopter niche and never crosses to mainstream travelers.
2. Hotel/hospitality regulation in major cities restricts or bans the model before it reaches scale.
