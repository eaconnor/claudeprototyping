Gate 1 is unverified — problem statement is tagged [A] technology-push thesis with no sourced user pain point. Council voices below are commentary against an assumed problem, not analysis against a verified one.

### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- Glass bundles four unranked jobs (notifications, photos, navigation, translation) with no evidence any of them fires an existing solution, so its strongest objection is that a product without a validated primary job cannot be hired for anything specific.

### Service Blueprint -- The frontstage recording indicator is invisible to bystanders while the backstage capture is always-on, and the strongest objection is that this is a blueprint with no consent touchpoint for the non-customer actor (the bystander) who bears the cost.

### Self-Determination Theory -- The $1,500 price and Explorer-only access support competence/autonomy for the wearer but the design supports zero relatedness for the bystander, whose objection is that a device optimizing one party's agency by removing another party's is not a trust model, it's an externality.

### Disruptive Innovation -- This reads as a sustaining bet dressed as disruption (higher price, lower simplicity, targeting developers not underserved customers), and its strongest objection is that Christensen's model requires the new entrant to be simpler/cheaper for a non-consumer, which $1,500 Explorer Edition inverts.

### Value Chain -- Glass tries to insert at the point-of-capture layer of the personal-data value chain ahead of the phone, and the strongest objection is that inserting there without bystander consent infrastructure means the margin gain for N-able-style ecosystem control is captured before the social-license cost is priced in at all.

> Disruptive Innovation and Value Chain diverge on what kind of bet this is — Disruptive Innovation reads it as mispriced-for-disruption, Value Chain reads it as correctly-positioned-but-uncosted; this is a decision about what "success" means here, not a gap to reconcile.
