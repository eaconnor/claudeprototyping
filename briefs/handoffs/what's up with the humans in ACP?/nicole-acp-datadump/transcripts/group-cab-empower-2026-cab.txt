Key Decisions

Detailed Minutes

[18:57-19:51] A strategy for identifying internal AI utilization involves using tools like Prompt,
which offer assessment and ongoing service packages.
-The vendor "Prompt" is developing a two-track MSP model: an assessment pack and an
ongoing service pack.
-This addresses a significant gap where companies are unaware of internal AI usage.
-The recommended positioning is not to police AI use but to identify "AI rockstars" and
find ways to make them more effective.
-An alternative, blunt-force method is to use existing tools like OpenDNS to generate
reports on the top AI models being used within the company. This requires no new
installations, as the data is likely already being collected.

[19:53-20:18] Cisco Umbrella is being used as a fast, attention-getting tool to identify internal
AI usage, similar to OpenDNS.
-This method, while a "blunt instrument," provides a quick way to get leadership's
attention regarding AI use.
-The conversation notes a link between this kind of "shadow IT" discovery and the
broader topic of generative AI.

[20:21-21:41] ServiceNow and Microsoft platforms are the primary tools used for client AI
orchestration, though custom development is often required.
-Microsoft's capabilities are often "oversold," necessitating custom agency work.
-Custom development is typically not greenfield but is built upon an established
development framework and standard.
-Back-end microservices are used, with a custom front-end built on top of the platform.
-This involves adding agents to the client's existing cloud environment, whether it's Azure
or AWS.
-A new initiative is underway to explore a "cloud infrastructure, superstructure, managed
agent infrastructure" to move away from locally running solutions.

[22:40-24:39] A discussion was held on the necessity and methodology of backing up AI
agents, concluding it's more of an application management issue than a traditional backup
problem.
-The need for backups arises because agents represent a layer of company data that
must be protected, especially those with company-specific prompts.
-User-created agents represent intellectual property (IP) that can be lost when an
employee leaves or if an inactive workspace (e.g., in Power Platform) is deleted.
-Configurations and attached workflows (e.g., in Co-pilot Studio) have been lost in the
past due to a failure to restore a deleted workspace within the 7-day window.
-The recommended approach is to manage agents like any other application, using a
CI/CD pipeline rather than traditional backup methods.
oGovernance can be implemented in platforms like Power Platform to prevent users from
creating agents outside of a managed process.
oA CI/CD process ensures agents are automatically pushed into Git repositories, allowing
for redeployment if they are destroyed, similar to how API servers are managed.

[24:39-25:23] The group discussed handling rollbacks for actions taken by AI agents,
concluding it relies on existing infrastructure protections and change management.
-For rollback actions an agent might take, the process should be part of a methodical
CI/CD pipeline and change management strategy.
-If an agent takes action on infrastructure (e.g., deleting a user), the hope is that existing
infrastructure backups and protections are already in place to handle restoration.
-The goal is to merge agent management and infrastructure protection into a more
cohesive strategy.

[25:32-26:50] AI projects are often perceived as lacking direct ROI, creating a challenge for
service providers to monetize their offerings.
-The industry is currently in the "trough of disillusionment" on the Gartner Hype Cycle for
AI.
-Many C-suite executives are questioning the value of their AI investments, as they have
spent significant money without seeing a clear return.
-The return is often framed as ROX (Return on Experience) rather than a direct, financial
ROI.
-This creates a key challenge for service providers: determining how to effectively
monetize and wrap services around AI.
-Current engagements are often project-based, typically involving a group of concepts.

[26:55-27:05] A question was raised regarding whether Co-pilot currently enables the backup

on.

[29:15-30:11] An AI adoption service for recurring revenue is being explored.

[30:11-31:50] A new commercial model for deployed AI agents is being tested to incentivize
client usage.
-The company has deployed proprietary agents to clients, similar to those used for
internal ticketing processes.
-The team is grappling with creating a commercial model that doesn't penalize clients for
increased usage, which is a departure from traditional automation pricing.
-The desired model ties pricing to the benefit the client receives, rather than transaction
volume, to encourage adoption.
-Current interim solution: After the initial release, the company is using a fixed-fee model
with time blocks (e.g., a set price per month for six months of management).
-The business case relies on the client realizing significant cost savings (e.g., reducing
headcount) over the trial period, which justifies the ongoing cost for both parties.
-This model is currently in an experimental phase with clients since January, and its long-
term success is unknown.

[33:04-34:04] A discussion was initiated on security vulnerabilities and response
mechanisms in an AI-driven world.
-A high-level question was posed about potential AI-related attacks.
-Supply chain security, potentially referring to the software bill of materials (SBOM), was
mentioned as a concern.
-The group has internal signals to detect an attack, but a response still requires manual
intervention from a small number of people to authorize a system shutdown.

[34:33-37:15] The group debated the safety of allowing AI agents to perform non-
deterministic execution versus deterministic automation.
-It was asserted that workflows that can be solved deterministically are safe to fully
automate.
-A strong reluctance was expressed towards allowing non-deterministic workflows to end
in an execution result, regardless of the perceived risk level.
-The risk is dependent on the tools and capabilities given to the agent; a "read-only"
agent is acceptable, but one with execution capabilities is considered scary.
-A distinction was made between a non-deterministic agent making a decision (e.g.,
choosing to reboot a machine based on loose guidelines) and an agent executing a pre-
defined deterministic workflow where a human has already established the rules (e.g., "if X, Y,
and Z are true, then reboot").
-The consensus is that deterministic automation, where the logic is pre-defined by a
human, is safe and is what is commonly practiced. Truly non-deterministic execution by an
agent is viewed as risky because the outcome is unpredictable.

[37:15-38:17] An example of a deterministic workflow for rebooting a machine was provided.
-A system like Moveworks can process an intent to reboot a specific computer.
-It performs a deterministic check to verify the requesting user is authorized based on
CMDB data in ServiceNow.
-If the authorization check passes, the reboot command is executed via an agent. If it
fails, the user is informed they are not authorized.
-This process is not considered truly "agentic" in the non-deterministic sense, but rather a
standard, rule-based automation.

[38:17-40:07] The group discussed examples of semi-supervised agentic workflows currently
in use.
-One participant described an agentic process for error monitoring where, if a specific
type of error is detected, the agent can perform steps that result in creating a pull request
with a code fix into an "edge" environment.
-Key Decision: The agent is not allowed to push directly to the production environment,
but it can make changes to the codebase in a pre-production environment without direct
human supervision at that step. A human supervises before the change moves to production.
-This is considered a step closer to autonomous operation but is not 100% unsupervised.

[40:10-41:03] A cost-efficiency strategy for agentic workflows involves combining pattern
matching with deterministic automation.
-An agent first analyzes an inbound ticket or problem to see if it matches a known pattern.
-If a match is found, the agent runs a purely deterministic and token-efficient automation
workflow, pending human approval of the match's accuracy.
-If the agent cannot find a match ("I've never seen this before"), it then invokes other,
more expensive, "thinking" agents to devise a solution.
-The primary driver for this two-step approach was cost-efficiency (token usage), not just
safety.

[41:07-43:03] A three-agent model is being used for Tier 1 triage in a Security Operations
Center (SOC).
-The process involves three agent types:
o
4. Triage Agent: The initial agent that performs the work.
o
5. QA Agent: An agent that double-checks the work of the first agent.
o
6. Supervisor Agent: An agent that checks for any out-of-bounds actions and provides the final
output.
-This structure is used for rudimentary tasks, such as investigating "impossible travel"
alerts where a user's location appears anomalous.
-It was confirmed that supervisors and monitors are deployed to oversee the actions of AI
agents, especially in scenarios like a disgruntled employee misusing the system.

[43:09-44:48] The discussion touched on the appropriate level of trust for AI in critical
decision-making, such as in healthcare.
-An example was given of a system in a medical suite like Epic, where AI could act as a
co-pilot for a doctor.
-The AI might notice a patient's allergy to a medication (e.g., ibuprofen) in the case notes
and flag it for the doctor, who may not have noticed it immediately.
-A consensus was expressed that the final decision, such as what medication to
prescribe, must still reside with the human professional.
-A key challenge identified is the difficulty in identifying which manual processes are good
candidates for automation, as technicians are often accustomed to their workflows and don't
proactively flag them.
-A suggestion was made for a system layer that could monitor ticketing or other systems
to identify repetitive issues that are consistently resolved the same way, recommending them
as candidates for AI automation.

[44:48-45:20] An example of a read-only AI agent for identifying automation opportunities
was highlighted.
-An AI agent was created to identify tickets that are "impossible to close," surfacing these
as potential areas for process improvement or automation.
-This was noted as a good example of a "read-only" use case for finding valuable
insights.

[47:31-48:24] The challenge of moving too slowly in AI adoption was identified as a primary
concern.
-When asked what to avoid, "being too slow" was stated as the number one concern.
-The scale of operations (e.g., two people running 400 agents) was mentioned to
underscore the massive potential impact and risk, highlighting the need for careful but timely
action.

[48:24-49:56] The organization's goals for AI implementation focus on value demonstration,
operational efficiency, and customization.
-The primary goal is to make operational complexity "real life" by not just automating
existing work but finding fundamentally better ways of operating.
-Value Ledger: A "value ledger" will be created for all AI-driven automation. This ledger
will quantify the value generated (e.g., hours saved, money saved) to provide clear visibility
and justification for AI initiatives.
-Beyond Real-Time: The belief is that "real-time is not fast enough," and operations need
to be predictive to stay ahead of issues.
-On-Demand Customization: The future of services should be customized and built live
on-demand, moving away from standardized offerings. The technology (e.g., HTML5 in the
browser) exists to create and present data visualizations in real time.
-This overall goal is being translated by each product line into specific applications and
build plans for their respective products.

[50:04-50:55] An "AI expert" is proposed as a solution to provide business outcome

[50:57-52:02] The "Business Expert" is presented as an example of a controversial but

[53:47-54:40] A discussion was initiated on identifying high-value, automatable use cases for

-The goal is to increase the ratio of devices managed per L1 technician, which currently

-The focus should be on identifying specific activities that can be automated to improve

[54:42-56:14] The conversation shifted to whether automation should target L1 tasks or more

-The concept of an "onboarding agent" was discussed as a use case.

oDetecting specific applications and settings.
oIdentifying deviations from best practices across the customer base.
oAutomating the setup of custom thresholds and best practices to accelerate the initial

[56:14-57:00] The timing of the MCP server release was criticized as being too late.
