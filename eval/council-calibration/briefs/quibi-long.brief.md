The stated problem — a gap between free UGC (YouTube) and long-form premium (Netflix) for short, in-between moments — is asserted from executive experience, not sourced user research. ## Gate 2 — Right thing
Bet: a distinct content format (short "chapters," mobile-only, high production value) commands a subscription price existing free options don't. [A] $1.8B raised pre-launch on this thesis. [D]

## Gate 3 — Built right
Mobile-only — no TV app, no web app, no casting to a living room screen at launch. [A] Launch date lands during a period of widespread stay-at-home orders, eliminating the commute occasion the product is built around. [D — external shock, unforeseen at brief-writing time but material to Gate 3 timing risk]

## Failure modes
1. Mobile-only distribution excludes at-home and TV viewing entirely, cutting off the majority of a typical household's screen time.
2. No clearly underserved job — YouTube and podcasts already fill commute-gap viewing for free; premium pricing has no differentiated value to point to.
