## Gate 1 — Problem
The problem — that the dominant social network's single-audience sharing model is a real pain point — is asserted internally, not sourced from research on users who are dissatisfied enough to switch or dual-use. [A] No data cited on how many users actively want granular circles versus tolerate current sharing norms. [?]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Bet: integrating a new social layer across existing widely-used products (mail, video, photos) will seed adoption faster than a standalone app could. [A] Sign-up is required to use several existing products, rather than purely opt-in. [D]

## Gate 3 — Built right
Mandatory integration means account creation numbers will include users who did not choose the social product for its own sake. [D] No standalone metric distinguishes forced sign-ups from users who post, comment, or return without a product-integration trigger. [?]

## Failure modes
1. Forced integration inflates "user" counts without reflecting real engagement, masking whether anyone actually wants the product — a vanity metric risk.
2. Network effects are already locked in by the dominant competitor; a late entrant needs a genuinely distinct reason to switch or dual-use, not just feature parity plus integration.
