## Org size distribution (p.32, Fig. 25)
Kaseya's own size bands: ≤10 employees = "small scale," 11–50 = "mid-scale," 50+ = "large scale."

| Employee count | % of respondents |
|---|---|
| Less than 5 | 17% |
| 5–10 | 14% |
| 11–25 | 18% |
| 26–50 | 15% |
| 51–100 | 11% |

Roll-up: **31% small-scale (≤10 employees)**, **33% mid-scale (11–50)**, **36% large-scale (50+)**. [CS: VERIFIED]

Years in business (p.32, Fig. 24): 46% have operated 16+ years; only 7% are under 3 years old. [CS: VERIFIED] — this is a mature-firm-skewed sample, likely to over-represent structured orgs relative to the true population of MSPs (younger/smaller shops are harder to reach via vendor survey panels).

## Respondent role split (p.33, Fig. 27)
> "Executives account for 43% of respondents, including owners, vice presidents and partners. The remaining 57% represent technical and operational roles such as IT managers, supervisors, project managers, network engineers, system administrators, and marketing and sales staff."

- **Executives (43%):** MSP owner/partner, VP/director
- **"Technicians" (57%):** IT manager/supervisor, project manager/network engineer, system administrator or IT technician, marketing and sales

[CS: VERIFIED] — quoted directly. [ASSUMPTION — Kaseya's, not Beth's or mine]: Kaseya's binary lumps IT managers/supervisors and project managers into the same bucket as hands-on system administrators and marketing/sales staff. That's a wide net labeled "Technicians." It means Kaseya's own instrument cannot distinguish "the person who racks a switch" from "the person who owns a P&L for a service line" — both land in the 57% bucket. **This directly undercuts any clean two-camp read of "technician vs. economic buyer": Kaseya's own taxonomy already blends operational and decision-adjacent roles under one label.**

## Talent/staffing signal (p.4, p.28)
- 16% of MSPs report difficulty hiring skilled technicians in 2026, up from 9% in 2025. [CS: VERIFIED]
- 83% say IT management tools "significantly enhance operational efficiency" — satisfaction with tools is rising while staffing capacity is becoming the binding constraint. [CS: VERIFIED]
- This is Kaseya's own framing that the "tool problem" is fading relative to the "people problem" — it does not address who inside the MSP evaluates/buys those tools.

## What this data does NOT answer
No question in this survey asks "who decided to purchase your current RMM/PSA/security stack" or "is the person using the tool daily the same person who approved the spend." The 43/57 executive/technician split is a *respondent demographic* stat (who filled out this survey), not a *purchasing authority* stat. Treating it as a proxy for buying structure requires an inferential leap Kaseya doesn't make and I won't make for them. [CS: LOW if used as a buying-decision proxy]
