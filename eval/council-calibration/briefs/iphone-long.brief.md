## Gate 1 — Problem
Existing smartphones are built for enterprise use cases (email, calendar) with hardware (stylus, keyboard) optimized for precision input, not media or web browsing. [D] No dominant device treats the phone as a general consumer computer. [A]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Bet: consumers want one device that replaces phone + iPod + a "internet communicator," and will accept an all-touch interface to get it. [A] No prior product has validated all-touch typing at scale. [?]

## Gate 3 — Built right
No physical keyboard — all input via software touch keyboard. [D] No third-party app store at launch; only Apple's own applications. [A] Exclusive single-carrier deal (AT&T) rather than multi-carrier availability. [A]

## Failure modes
1. Touch keyboard typing accuracy is rejected by users accustomed to physical keys, especially for email-heavy use.
2. Closed platform with no third-party apps limits utility to what Apple ships itself, capping appeal beyond early adopters.
