## Direct Quotes

> Steve Ballmer, USA Today interview, April 29, 2007: "There's no chance that the iPhone is going to get any significant market share. No chance. It's a $500 subsidized item." **[CS: HIGH — quote widely and consistently cited with exact date across independent sources including TechRadar, MacRumors, BGR; original USA Today piece not directly fetchable, 403'd]**

> Jim Balsillie (RIM co-CEO), via Reuters, Feb 12, 2007, reported by Wojtek Dabrowski, republished by MacDailyNews same day: "It's kind of one more entrant into an already very busy space with lots of choice for consumers. But in terms of a sort of a sea-change for BlackBerry, I would think that's overstating it." **[CS: HIGH — sourced to a named Reuters reporter and a same-day republication, dated]**

> Larry Conlee (RIM COO), on the iPhone circa Jan 2007, quoted in the book *Losing the Signal* (McNish & Silcoff), via Forbes 2015: "It wasn't secure. It had rapid battery drain and a lousy [digital] keyboard." **[CS: MEDIUM — secondhand account reconstructed years later for a book, not a verified real-time quote; treat as recollection]**

> Jim Balsillie / Mike Lazaridis internal exchange after watching the Jobs keynote, per *Losing the Signal* via Forbes — Balsillie: "It's OK — we'll be fine." Lazaridis: "These guys are really, really good. This is different." **[CS: MEDIUM — book reconstruction of a private conversation, not a document from 2007 itself]**

> John C. Dvorak, MarketWatch, March 28, 2007 ("Apple should pull the plug on the iPhone"): "It's the loyalists who keep promoting this device as if it is going to be anything other than another phone in a crowded market." "These phones go in and out of style so fast that unless Apple has half a dozen variants in the pipeline, its phone, even if immediately successful, will be passé within 3 months." "If it's smart it will call the iPhone a 'reference design' and pass it to some suckers to build with someone else's marketing budget." **[CS: HIGH — quotes consistently reproduced across TechCrunch's March 29, 2007 same-week writeup and MacDailyNews' same-week writeup; original MarketWatch column behind paywall/403]**

> Seth Porges, TechCrunch, June 2007 ("We Predict the iPhone Will Bomb"): the virtual keyboard "will be about as useful for tapping out emails and text messages as a rotary phone" — also foresaw cracked screens, poor battery life, and bugs from a rushed launch. **[CS: HIGH — Porges later confirmed in interviews the piece was his, though he disputed that the headline "Will Bomb" was added by an editor without his approval — note this nuance]**

> Slashdot thread reacting same-day to Steve Jobs' Macworld announcement (Jan 9, 2007) that no native third-party apps would be allowed, thread dated Jan 12, 2007: commenter "Wyldeone" called Jobs' justification "absurd," criticizing Apple's "closed, expensive development platform" versus open competitor SDKs; commenter "Fnkmaster" called the no-apps policy a "deal-breaker." **[CS: MEDIUM — real dated forum commentary, but forum comments are lower-authority than press/exec quotes; useful as contemporaneous public sentiment, not as an "expert" quote]**

> Steve Jobs himself, Macworld keynote Jan 9, 2007 / January 2007 press remarks, naming the platform risk from Apple's own side: "These are devices that need to work, and you can't do that if you load any software on them." "Cingular doesn't want to see their West Coast network go down because some application messed up." **[CS: MEDIUM — quote reproduced via Slashdot's Jan 12, 2007 story citing the original announcement; not independently verified against a primary transcript in this pass]**
