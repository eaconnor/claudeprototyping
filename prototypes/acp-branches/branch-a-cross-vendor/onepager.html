The demand signal is loud

Why it's hard

The most defensible comparison for a client is also the legally hardest to assemble. Cross-vendor normalization is not started, and pulling competitors' data into a client-facing artifact raises a data-egress question no one has answered yet.

3

The lever that changes the odds

Buy the normalization moat — it's an existing small-player category, not a multi-quarter build. Partner past the egress wall — a vendor relationship turns a ToS blocker into a marketplace integration.

What N-able already has

First-party telemetry across Adlumin, Cove, and UEM is already a normalized source N-able controls. "Measured" claims are defensible on day one for N-able-native clients — before any acquisition closes.

The N-able superpower isn't the AI. It's the telemetry.

Adlumin + Cove + UEM give N-able a full-stack, first-party telemetry base that no competitor bundle matches. The acquisition or partnership extends that moat across the rest of the stack — but the moat itself already exists, and it's the reason this package is winnable despite starting from the lowest odds in the portfolio.

The ask

Fund the two cheap tests before funding the build: a Q1 legal read on cross-vendor data egress, and a Q2 operator study on whether MSPs want less verification work, not just more output.
