### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- The core job hired ("kill 8 minutes in line") is already served free by YouTube/TikTok, and the brief names no evidence anyone fires that job for a paid alternative.

### Service Blueprint -- Mobile-only at launch collapses frontstage viewing options to a single screen with no backstage fallback (no TV, no cast), and the brief doesn't address what happens to the service model when the commute occasion itself vanishes.

### Self-Determination Theory -- Chaptered 7-10 min format may serve competence (easy to finish) but undermines autonomy by dictating where/how content can be watched, and the brief has no user evidence this trade was ever tested against real viewers.

### Disruptive Innovation -- This reads as sustaining-premium dressed as disruptive-occasion (high production value, subscription price) rather than a low-end disruption, and it defends nothing an incumbent needs to worry about since free UGC already owns the low end.

### Value Chain -- $1.8B was committed to a distribution chokepoint (mobile-only, in-between moments) that a single external shock (stay-at-home orders) can eliminate overnight, and the brief treats that timing risk as unforeseeable rather than a margin-of-safety failure in the bet itself.

> Disruptive Innovation and Value Chain converge on the same root cause (bet concentrated on one occasion, no hedge) but Jobs-to-Be-Done and Self-Determination Theory diverge on whether the format itself (short chapters) is the flaw versus the distribution constraint (mobile-only) being the flaw — this is a decision, not a gap.
