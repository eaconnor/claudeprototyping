Every agent's AEIOU-Users cell and Human Factors-Social/Cultural cell land in the same place: the demand corpus studied buyers (owners, VPs) and the agents will be operated by a different person (tech, dispatcher, monitoring analyst, security facilitator). The gap is not incidental — the four agents that ranked below QBR are the agents most likely to be operated by the non-buyer. An Alert Flood Suppression log designed for a VP is not a product; a suppression log designed for the dispatcher who reviews it at 8am is. Route to: plan the next interview round around the operator persona, not additional buyer validation. Decision: Beth/Meaghan.

### Finding 2: The signal format is the single undefined dependency that blocks all four agents' Exit/Extend stages.
Alert Flood Suppression (Exit), Incident Response RCA (Extend), Continuous Environment Health (Exit), and Security Tabletop (Extend) all require a typed signal the QBR coworker can consume. In every case, the signal format is [A]/[?]. This is not four separate holes — it is one shared interface that does not exist. The department thesis depends on it. Route to: a signal-format design spike before any agent builds an Exit path. Decision: Beth/Meaghan/Eng.

### Finding 3: Cultural identity is the sleeper human factor in every agent, and the risk intensifies as the agent acts rather than reports.
QBR map: cultural competence/identity is the sleeper (the MSP as strategist). Alert Flood Suppression: vigilant-sentinel identity. Incident Response RCA: investigator identity. Continuous Environment Health: proactive-monitor identity. Security Tabletop: credible-security-facilitator identity. The pattern across all five agents: **the more the coworker does the thing the MSP's professional identity is built on, the higher the cultural risk**. And the risk intensifies along the interact-class axis: report-only (QBR, RCA) < recommend-then-act (Alert Flood, Env Health) < live-facilitate (Tabletop). The live-facilitate class carries the highest cultural risk because the MSP's professional identity as a facilitator is performed in front of a client room. This is the one human factor with zero data across all five agents. Route to: include identity/competence probes in the next operator research round. Decision: Beth.

### Finding 4: The 4As all fail at Affordability for the two recommend-then-act agents, and the failure is structural, not pricing.
Alert Flood Suppression and Continuous Environment Health both face the "build it into the RMM" ceiling [R] as the strongest resistance signal. This is not a pricing problem — it is a category-definition problem: buyers define these capabilities as features of tools they already pay for. The only exit from this ceiling is the department thesis itself (the value is the cross-coworker signal, not the suppression or health check alone). But the department thesis depends on the signal format (Finding 2) existing. The two structural problems are entangled: until the signal format exists, the department value proposition cannot be demonstrated, and until the department value can be demonstrated, the free-in-RMM ceiling cannot be cleared. Route to Beth/Meaghan/Nicole as the framing and pricing decision the four maps cannot resolve.

### Cross-agent finding the per-agent view cannot see: The interaction-class axis also maps to the liability axis.
Report-only agents (QBR, RCA) carry liability in what they say (a wrong number, a wrong root cause narrated confidently). Recommend-then-act agents (Alert Flood, Env Health) carry liability in what they do (a suppressed critical, a missed drift). Live-facilitate (Tabletop) carries liability in what the artifact implies (passed ≠ covered). The trust kit that the product-experience-research describes (drillable provenance, visible confidence, reviewable log, human review gate) addresses the report-only liability class. **It does not fully address the do-liability or the implication-liability.** A reviewable log can show that the suppression rule fired correctly; it cannot prevent the rule from being wrong. A TabletopReport can state "passed ≠ covered"; it cannot control how a client reads it. The trust kit needs at least two additional elements: (1) a rate-of-correctness record for recommend-then-act agents (the suppression log must show the track record, not just the action); (2) explicit legal framing in the Tabletop artifact. Route to Beth.
