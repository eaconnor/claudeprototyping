Tier: MEAL

### Jobs-to-Be-Done -- Webvan served the functional job "get groceries without a store trip" but engineered for an assumed job of "replace all grocery retail infrastructure nationally," and the strongest objection is that it over-built capacity for a job scale no customer had actually hired anyone to do yet [CS: HIGH].

### Service Blueprint -- the frontstage (a simple ordering website) hid a backstage of custom-built automated warehouses and owned delivery fleets across multiple cities simultaneously, and the strongest objection is that the brief names none of the backstage failure points — vendor risk, warehouse automation vendor (Bechtel) dependency, or who absorbs cost when the automation doesn't hit throughput targets [CS: HIGH].

### Self-Determination Theory -- the ordering experience likely supported user autonomy and competence (pick items, get them delivered) but offered little relatedness since it severed the customer from any store or community touchpoint, and the strongest objection is that SDT has almost nothing to say about why this venture failed because the failure was capital structure and operations, not user psychology.

### Disruptive Innovation -- Webvan was sustaining/premium innovation masquerading as disruption, entering with more expensive infrastructure rather than a simpler foothold product, and the strongest objection is that this is the inverse of the disruptive playbook — it attacked incumbents from above with heavier capex, not from below with something cheaper and worse, so it had no low-end beachhead to defend if the model failed [CS: HIGH].

### Value Chain -- Webvan vertically integrated the entire grocery value chain from procurement to last-mile rather than inserting at a single chokepoint, and the strongest objection is that full vertical integration ahead of proven demand meant it absorbed 100% of margin risk with none of the scale needed to amortize the capex, guaranteeing negative margin at the volumes it actually achieved [CS: HIGH].

> Self-Determination Theory and Value Chain/Disruptive Innovation diverge sharply here: SDT would read the user-facing experience as sound (autonomy and competence intact), while the other two frameworks locate total failure at the business-model layer regardless of user experience quality — this is a decision about which layer of the brief actually needs scrutiny, not a gap to resolve.
