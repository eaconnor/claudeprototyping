A QBR coworker that tells one story across every vendor in the stack

Most MSPs run 6–8 competing tools per client and cannot tell a single comparable story from them. This concept asks: what would it look like if an AI coworker normalized that whole mixed-vendor stack into one client-ready quarterly narrative — and what would it take, legally and technically, to get there?

For the board

The problem

An MSP can't prove its value in one narrative because the evidence lives in 6+ vendor tools that don't talk to each other. The most convincing comparison — across the whole stack, not just N-able's slice — is also the one that's hardest to legally assemble.

The value prop

For MSPs who assemble quarterly reviews by hand: a client-ready, evidence-tagged narrative across the full vendor stack in under 20 minutes — not another single-vendor dashboard, but one normalized story with defensibility built in.

Why now

This is the #1 requested capability across all three Guidepoint research instruments — MSPs call it "the holy grail." No incumbent ships it. But demand has only been tested for the destination (a cross-stack QBR), never for the mechanism (AI drafts, human edits).

Bradley rail — what this is testing

Tier 0 · Concept, reacting only

This sketch is testing whether the board finds the cross-vendor QBR narrative compelling enough to justify examining the buy/partner path — not whether the normalization engine should be built in-house. No build commitment is implied by this artifact.

What's strong here

  • #1 demand signal across all three Guidepoint research instruments — MSPs unprompted call this "the holy grail."
  • The two things that kill it (normalization, egress) are buy/partner lines, not build lines — which is exactly why the fleet synthesis calls this the most winnable package despite the lowest floor.
  • N-able's first-party telemetry (Adlumin + Cove + UEM) is already a normalized source — "Measured" tags are defensible for N-able-native clients today, with no acquisition required.

What will hurt you

  • Demand was tested for the destination (a cross-stack QBR), never for the mechanism (AI drafts, human edits) — that's a known hole.
  • Cross-vendor data egress is legally unexamined. This is the single most decisive open test in the whole portfolio.
  • The three riskiest assumptions are correlated, so the ~0.43% floor behaves like a ceiling, not a floor, until at least one is tested.
Routing

The data-egress legal call (can N-able pull competitors' data into a client-facing artifact?) is not a design or product decision — it belongs with Legal/Compliance, informed by Product's Q1 legal read. Design and Product hold the narrative and tiering pattern; Legal holds go/no-go on the mechanism.
