What David Liked About Ask N‑zo 
1. Strong value for QBRs and customer‑facing reporting
David consistently pointed to Quarterly Business Reviews and client summaries as the clearest win for N‑zo when the data is accurate.
What he liked
Fast creation of client‑ready narratives
Clear summaries that would normally take significant manual effort
Ability to frame insights as recommendations without being salesy
Quotes
“I was just giving scenarios like, OK, I’m preparing for quarterly business review and create report for me so that I can give it to client.”
“I actually told it, you know, don’t be salesy but at the same time give constructive information that could be used as kind of almost natural recommendations… It actually did a good job.”
“It would have taken me easily an hour, hour and a half to put something like this together, and I managed to do it in like 15–20 minutes.”
Use case
QBR narrative generation
Client‑facing summaries with light upsell framing
Business reviews prepared by non‑technical users
2. Clear time savings, even in early form
David emphasized that even with current limitations, N‑zo delivered meaningful time efficiency for certain workflows.
Quotes
“Definitely I saw a lot of value just in that.”
“It would have taken me easily an hour, hour and a half… and I managed to do it in like 15–20 minutes.”
Use case
Rapid report drafting
First‑pass analysis before refinement
Reducing manual writing and formatting effort
3. Easy to use and intuitive
David rated N‑zo highly for usability and onboarding, separating experience quality from data limitations.
Quotes
“It was at least four, I would say… it was very intuitive.”
“It didn’t take much time for me to kind of figure out how to make it useful.”
Use case
Fast adoption without training overhead
Accessible for business users, not just engineers
4. Clear fit for different user personas
David explicitly distinguished between where Ask N‑zo works well today and where it does not yet.
What he liked
Recognized that Ask N‑zo has a strong role for business and reporting users
Saw it as one tool in a broader toolbox, not a one‑size‑fits‑all solution
Quotes
“Enzo has its place… it just depends what you’re trying to achieve.”
“There is a user set within your organization who would get value out of it, and then a user set… that would get more value out of MCP.”
Use case
Business reviews
Executive summaries
Non‑engineering operational insights

While this is technically adjacent to Ask N‑zo, David repeatedly connected his excitement about MCP to unlocking much greater value from N‑zo’s data.

6. Willingness to adopt Ask N‑zo when GA
Despite being candid about limitations, David was clear about adoption intent.
Quote
“Yeah, of course. Yeah, definitely have its place.” (when asked if they would adopt Ask N‑zo at GA)
Summary Insight
David sees Ask N‑zo as already valuable for business reviews, reporting, and narrative generation, with clear time savings and strong usability. He does not view it as a full technician or NOC solution yet, but he sees MCP as the bridge that could turn N‑zo from a helpful copilot into a platform that materially moves the needle for MSP operations.
 
WHAT CAN BE IMPROVED
 
1. Data accuracy and completeness (top priority)
What needs improvement

David’s biggest issue was not directionality, but trust. When the data is wrong or incomplete, it breaks the workflow because everything must be double‑checked.
Quotes
“It’s a trust thing… if it gives like wrong number… then they need to kind of double check and scrutinize everything.”
“That becomes counterproductive because then it’s almost like it takes as much time to double check.”
“It’s kind of this 90 percent plus good job, but then… yeah, not 100%.”
Concrete example
N‑zo reported all devices as Windows 10 and zero Windows 11 devices, which did not match their environment.
“Which clearly does not match our current environment where Windows 11 is actually the majority.”
Implication
For customer‑facing use cases, accuracy needs to be high‑confidence, not “mostly right.”
2. Full device coverage and ingestion gaps
What needs improvement

David called out that N‑zo was clearly not reading the full device set, which directly contributed to inaccurate results.
Quotes
“When we read in the device list, we clearly did not read in all 1300.”
“That’s something… we’ll go investigate what happened there.”
Implication
Partial ingestion undermines analytics, summaries, and reports.
MSPs need confidence that all managed devices are represented, not a subset.
3. Structured outputs (CSV, Word, files)
What needs improvement

Chat‑only output limits real‑world use. David repeatedly emphasized the need for structured, exportable formats.
Quotes
“He wanted CSV file as an output and I believe that you cannot do that yet.”
“Chat just chatting is not enough. Typically analysis needs to be more structured.”
“If it gave you an output as a file, then that information is more easily actionable.”
“Even like Word document for example… it doesn’t need to be fancy formatting.”
Implication
N‑zo should support:
CSV for analysis
Word or document output for QBRs
Files that can drop directly into existing workflows
4. Access to checks, alerts, and operational data
What needs improvement

N‑zo is currently limited for engineers because it cannot reason over checks, alerts, or operational signals.
Quotes
“It just doesn’t have access to checks and things like that.”
“You cannot ask Enzo… what I need to do as a NOC engineer.”
“It’s very limited to what it can help you with as an engineer.”
Implication
Without checks, alerts, patch, and backup context, N‑zo can’t support:
NOC workflows
Proactive maintenance
Daily technician prioritization
5. Actionable prioritization, not just information
What needs improvement

David doesn’t just want answers. He wants distilled insight and prioritization.
Quotes
“A lot of it is not really even fixing anything, it’s just going through different screens, menus, looking for false positives.”
“If there was a way for it to distill it to insights… it gives you actually almost like an action list.”
“Today, that’s a priority based on what’s going on with alerts.”
Implication
High value comes from:
Noise reduction
Ranked action lists
Clear “what should I work on today” guidance
6. Technician and NOC efficiency use cases
What needs improvement

David described a very specific time sink in NOC operations that N‑zo does not yet address.
Quotes
“We have a dedicated person that spends probably 20 hours a week… going through alerts.”
“80–90% of that time is just checking this, checking that.”
“That work is definitely automatable because it’s just cutting from the noise.”
Implication
This is a clear opportunity for:
Proactive maintenance insights
Cross‑client alert synthesis
AI‑generated daily work plans
7. ROI clarity and monetization readiness
What needs improvement

David was candid that today’s value is incremental, not yet transformational.
Quotes
“At the moment… it’s kind of just marginal improvements.”
“There needs to be something where… I can quantify it.”
“Not just a marginal difference… it needs to move the needle.”
Implication
To justify premium pricing or expansion:
Clear efficiency gains
Measurable time savings
Technician capacity increases
