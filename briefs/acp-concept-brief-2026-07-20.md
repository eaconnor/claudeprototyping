## THE FIVE

### 1 · QBR — N-able Native (Branch B)

**Why good, from every direction:**
- **Demand:** rides the single strongest signal in the whole corpus — QBR ranks #1 across three independent instruments (interviews, forced-rank survey, per-agent value survey).
- **Superpower fit:** Adlumin MDR telemetry, Cove DR/backup data, and SOC pattern data are first-party and N-able-only — "Measured" tier claims are defensible day one, no acquisition needed.
- **Feasibility:** rides the Runbook Engine (49/49 built) and Value Ledger (52/52 built) — no cross-vendor normalization dependency at all, which is the one genuinely hard, multi-quarter build everything else on this list has to solve for.
- **Viability:** legally cleaner than any cross-vendor variant — no ToS/DPA question to resolve first.
- **Risk register (canonical, live):** the honest gate is whether the *narrowed* job (N-able-stack-only) was ever independently demanded — it wasn't; the research asked about cross-vendor. North Star rates it "near-term lead," naive floor ~0.9% — roughly double Branch A's.
- **Next question:** does the operator distinguish "N-able-native QBR" from "the real thing," or do they just want QBR drafted at all? One operator session answers it.

### 2 · QBR — Cross-Vendor (Branch A)

**Why good, from every direction:**
- **Demand:** same #1×3 signal as Branch B, but at the actual scope the demand was measured for — cross-stack. Kenneth May: "Cross-stack QBR is the holy grail."
- **Superpower fit:** same first-party telemetry stack as Branch B, plus the normalization moat *if bought or partnered* rather than built — a real, existing acquisition category (analogous to Plaid in fintech).
- **Feasibility:** the one hard line — cross-vendor normalization — is "Not started," but rated HIGH doability *via acquisition/partnership*, not build. The legal question (Q1) is a days-long review, not a study.
- **Viability:** bigger prize than Branch B if it clears — the full cross-stack story is what justifies premium pricing over a per-tech-tool floor.
- **Risk register:** naive floor ~0.43–0.64% across two separate modeling runs — lower than Branch B specifically because of the legal/normalization stack, not because demand is weaker.
- **Next question:** the legal/DPA read (Q1) and one competitor ToS read — the single most decisive, cheapest test in the entire portfolio per the original fleet synthesis.

### 4 · vCISO/vCIO Packaging — flat-fee model specifically

**Why good, from every direction:**
- **Demand:** the underlying jobs (vCISO/vCIO) are real per-interview ("invisible competence is the job"), though the *packaging* itself is untested — no MSP has been asked if they'd buy and resell it this way.
- **The real lever:** this isn't one concept, it's a pricing-mechanism choice, and the choice matters enormously — revenue-share pricing survives at ~0.02% (dies at the "what do you charge your client" disclosure question); flat-fee-by-practice-size survives at ~14%, a 700x difference, because "how big is your practice" is an answerable onboarding question.
- **Superpower fit:** N-able's existing MSP channel relationships — no AI-native competitor has this distribution.
- **Feasibility:** genuinely new billing infrastructure required (a real, scoped, non-trivial build) — but it's a decision-and-build problem, not blocked on unsolved research, unlike #3.
- **Viability:** the only path to the larger revenue ceiling (share-of-services ~$4.3B vs. per-tool floor ~$1B) — **caveat: that $4.3B figure is not currently a linked, citable source per your own Secondary Research Repository rule; treat as directional only until re-sourced.**
- **Next question:** the positioning/message test — 5–8 real MSPs, would they buy and resell this — is nearly free and should run regardless of which billing model wins.

### 5 · Ticket Triage — reframed (instrumented on-ramp, or licensed to RMM/PSA vendors)

**Why good, from every direction:**
- **Feasibility — unmatched:** already built and shipped. Nothing else on this list can say that.
- **Demand — split, and that split is the whole story:** highest emotional intensity of anything tested (8.3/10, "give me that — I'll buy it today" — MSP-D) but ranks near-bottom on forced-rank priority (5.18 of ~8) — the classic table-stakes signature.
- **Superpower fit — currently weak:** the build's own critique names this directly — Triage isn't even in the build's researched top-5 catalog, and isn't the demanded job (QBR is, at #1).
- **Viability — the actual argument for keeping it in the top 5, not despite the weakness:** it's the cheapest fix in the entire portfolio. Two real options exist: (a) instrument the funnel (currently zero conversion tracking — nobody knows if it earns its keep) or (b) invert the "they'll build it free" threat into a license deal with the RMM/PSA vendors themselves, turning a competitive commodity into a distribution channel.
- **Risk register:** as currently sequenced (day-1, unfixed), the build's own analysis rates this a real weakness (BET1, p=0.35 — "contradicts own research rank; not in catalog").
- **Next question:** a message-order test (does leading with Triage anchor the whole platform as commodity) is cheap and answers the sequencing question directly.
