# ACP QBR Analyst — Concept MVPs (Geoffrey Moore value props)

Three viable concept shapes (from the research hub §TL;DR), each sketched as a **Moore positioning statement** + key tenets + expected value + why. Evidence-flagged throughout — Unknowns named, not fabricated (Bradley ROI discipline).

**Shared across all three:** the MVP *mechanics* are the same trust wedge (V1–V6: real Enter · one draft/one customer · drillable provenance · gap backstage · editable + one export + one audience mode · read-only). What differs is **data scope · economic model · who it's sold to.** Gate A + B select among them.

Moore template used:
> For **[target]** who **[need]**, the **[product]** is a **[category]** that **[key benefit]**. Unlike **[alternative]**, our product **[differentiator]**.

---

## A · Platform-Embedded QBR  *(market signal favors this)*

> **For** mid-size SMB MSPs already on the N-able stack (N-central/N-sight + Cove, 20–100 clients)
> **who** burn senior hours each quarter hand-assembling QBRs and default to ticket-noise instead of business outcomes,
> the **QBR Analyst** is an **AI coworker built into your N-able platform**
> that **turns the data already in your stack into a client-ready quarterly review in minutes — every number traceable.**
> **Unlike** standalone QBR/vCIO tools (ScalePad, myITprocess) that live outside your stack and make you reconcile by hand,
> **our product generates the review where your data already lives — no new integration, no export gymnastics.**

**MVP cut:** trust wedge over N-able-stack data first; on-demand; exec mode; PowerPoint export.

**Key tenets**
1. The data's already in your N-able stack — the QBR should write itself from it. [D — N-able owns N-central/Cove data cleanly]
2. Read-only, human-sends — zero governance conversation. [R]
3. Every number drillable to source — trust *is* the product. [D — demand]
4. Bundled / retention-defense economics — priced to protect the base, not as a new SKU. [market signal, [A]]

**Expected value**
- **Business:** stickier platform → retention defense against VC-rollups; fast time-to-value (own-stack data). *Unknown: retention-lift magnitude [?].*
- **User (MSP):** saves senior QBR-prep hours (direction hours→minutes [D]; magnitude [?]); looks credible/strategic to the client.
- **Unknown:** exact WTP contribution when bundled (vs. attach/retention) [?].

**Why:** consolidation trend + RMM-cannibalization make a standalone unwinnable; leaning on the platform converts the incumbent *headwind* (Profit Model) into *tailwinds* (Service · Channel · Product System — Doblin). Lowest WTP risk; normalization only needs own-stack (easier). **Moore chasm read:** leverage the installed base — the safest crossing.

**Bets on / riskiest dependency:** single-stack coverage being "enough" for a useful QBR (shares C's differentiation risk); C4 resolves toward *bundled*.

## B · Standalone Cross-Stack Product  *(highest reward, highest risk)*

> **For** any mid-size SMB MSP (20–100 clients), regardless of stack,
> **who** runs 6–8 disconnected tools and cannot get an apples-to-apples cross-vendor view for QBRs,
> the **QBR Analyst** is a **cross-stack AI reporting coworker**
> that **normalizes PSA + RMM + security + backup + identity from any vendor into one client-ready review.**
> **Unlike** ScalePad/Cognition360 (locked to their own suite or one PSA) or BI tools that don't reconcile across rivals,
> **our product is the only one that makes disconnected vendors apples-to-apples — the "holy grail" (Kenneth).**

**MVP cut:** trust wedge across one design-partner's *real* multi-vendor stack (the honest normalization test); on-demand; exec mode; export.

**Key tenets**
1. Vendor-agnostic normalization *is* the product. [R — demand / [?] — feasibility]
2. The network effect compounds — every connected stack improves mapping for all. [A — the moat]
3. Priced on value; sold beyond the N-able base.
4. Trust via drillable provenance; gaps backstage-only.

**Expected value**
- **Business:** new-market expansion (Ansoff Product-Development→Diversification); TAM beyond the N-able base; a moat a rollup can't buy (network effect). *Unknown: WTP above the free-in-RMM floor [?].*
- **User (MSP):** the cross-stack view they can't get anywhere; automates the *reconciliation judgment* — the expensive part of prep (JRNY-1).
- **Unknown:** whether cross-stack is the buyer's priority axis or a nice-to-have vs. consolidation (#7) [?].

**Why:** genuine white space (two independent absence-findings); highest reward. **Moore chasm read:** a whole-new-market play — the true crossing, and the true risk.

**Bets on / riskiest dependency:** #2 normalization works *at full scope* AND #5 WTP survives free-in-RMM — the two hardest, both OPEN. Exposure risk: ScalePad could fast-follow with 12,000 MSPs; venture money isn't on QBR-as-product yet.

## C · Narrow N-able-Stack QBR (beachhead)  *(fastest, smallest moat)*

> **For** N-able MSPs who want a trustworthy QBR now, over their N-able stack,
> **who** can't wait for cross-vendor normalization to be solved,
> the **QBR Analyst (Essentials)** is a **single-stack AI review**
> that **generates a client-ready QBR from N-central + Cove data in minutes.**
> **Unlike** cross-stack promises that require solving normalization first,
> **our product ships now on the data we already own cleanly — and earns the right to go cross-stack later.**

**MVP cut:** *this shape basically IS the MVP* — the trust wedge on clean own-stack data. Lowest build risk.

**Key tenets**
1. Ship the beachhead; don't wait for the moat.
2. Own-stack data is clean — feasibility is low-risk. [D]
3. Prove the trust moment (Enter + draft + provenance) on easy data first.
4. Explicitly a wedge, not the endgame.

**Expected value**
- **Business:** fastest time-to-market; lowest build risk; validates the trust hypothesis cheaply. *Unknown: whether single-stack is differentiated enough to matter [?] — the smallest moat.*
- **User (MSP):** a working QBR now — but limited to N-able-visible data (weak for mixed stacks).
- **Unknown:** adoption ceiling among MSPs with heavy non-N-able tooling [?].

**Why:** it's the Gate-A "NARROW" outcome — the honest fallback if normalization proves hard, *and* the safest MVP to learn from. **Moore chasm read:** the bowling-pin beachhead — win a defined niche first.

**Bets on / riskiest dependency:** #7 — if cross-stack *is* the priority axis, single-stack underwhelms exactly the buyers who care most.

## Cross-cut: how they relate

| | A · Platform-embedded | B · Standalone cross-stack | C · Narrow N-able-stack |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data scope | own stack + light 3rd-party | full cross-vendor | own stack only |
| Economics | bundled / retention | value-priced SKU | bundled / entry |
| Sold to | N-able base | any MSP | N-able base |
| Moat | platform lock + service | **normalization + network effect** | thin |
| Build risk | medium | high | **low** |
| WTP risk | **low** | high | low |
| Gate that decides it | B (pricing→bundled) | A+B (both must clear) | A (NARROW outcome) |

**Not mutually exclusive as a sequence:** C → A → B is a credible ladder — ship the beachhead (C), embed it in the platform (A), then extend to true cross-stack + network effect (B) once normalization and WTP are proven. That path spends the least before the riskiest unknowns are closed.

**Evidence flag (whole doc):** value *directions* are grounded ([R]/[D]); every value *magnitude* (retention lift, hours saved, WTP) is [?] — named, not fabricated. Closing them is the job of the quant survey + Gate A + Gate B + the conjoint.
