Brief: `newcoke-long.brief.md` — reformulated Coca-Cola replacing original recipe on blind-taste-test share loss.

Gate 1 status: Sourced but narrow. The problem statement is [D]-tagged (extensive blind taste-test data, correlated with share loss), but the measurement instrument itself is flagged as scoped to single-sip preference only, with no cited research on full-can, repeat, or ritual/identity consumption. This is a sourced problem with an unaddressed measurement-validity gap, not an unverified one. Council proceeds.

---

### Jobs-to-Be-Done — This brief hires a taste test to answer an identity question

The brief measures the functional job (does this liquid taste better in one sip) and extrapolates to a decision that touches the emotional and social jobs (this is *my* drink, this is what I order, this is what's in my fridge). Nothing in Gate 1 or Gate 3 asks what job the *original formula* was doing for loyal drinkers beyond flavor — ritual, memory, identity signaling are named in "What's missing" but never investigated.

The brief itself identifies the fired-solution risk inverted: the incumbent isn't getting fired by a competitor's superior job-fit, it's being voluntarily discontinued by its own maker while a competitor's product remains available to absorb any defectors created by the switch itself.

Single-sip blind testing structurally cannot detect job substitution over a "case in the pantry" or "order at the counter" job cycle — it tests preference divorced from the job's actual performance context.

**Strongest objection:** The brief treats "wins the taste test" and "does the job" as the same claim. They are not shown to be the same job. This is the exact gap Gate 2 names as [A] and then proceeds past anyway.

---

### Service Blueprint — No frontstage exists for the moment of loss

The brief's Gate 3 documents a backstage decision (full replacement, discontinue original) with zero frontstage design for how customers experience that discontinuation. There is no communication plan, no transition touchpoint, no channel for the customer reaction named in Gate 3 ("no consumer testing was conducted specifically on reaction to losing access to the original formula").

This is a support-process gap the brief itself flags but does not resource: when share-loss customers taste the new formula in a controlled blind test, that is a *research* frontstage. The retail shelf, on the day the original disappears, is a *product* frontstage with no research behind it at all.

The counter_metric ("loyalty/emotional attachment must not be damaged") is defined but has no corresponding blueprint step measuring it before or after launch — it's a stated constraint with no service touchpoint designed to protect it.

**Strongest objection:** A full-replacement launch model requires the heaviest backstage/frontstage coordination (single irreversible switch, no fallback) and the brief's evidence base is the thinnest exactly where that coordination matters most — the discontinuation moment.

---

### Self-Determination Theory — Autonomy is removed by design, not by accident

Full replacement (Gate 3) is a direct autonomy violation: the customer's choice to keep buying the original product is eliminated, not offered as an option. The brief considers and rejects the autonomy-preserving alternative (parallel launch) in Gate 2 without citing evidence for why it was rejected beyond "assumption that taste preference is the only variable that matters."

Competence is not implicated directly — this isn't a self-efficacy product — but relatedness is: the counter_metric names "loyalty/emotional attachment to the original formula" as a value at risk, which in SDT terms is a relatedness bond between customer and product/brand being severed by fiat with no consultation.

The idea_score is null and no consumer-facing test measured the reaction to losing the choice itself, only the reaction to the new formula's taste — so the autonomy cost of the launch model was never instrumented at all.

**Strongest objection:** The brief measures preference between two formulas but never measures preference for *having a choice* between two formulas — the actual autonomy variable the launch decision destroys is absent from every evidence source listed.

---

### Disruptive Innovation — This is defensive sustaining innovation, misclassified as decisive

This is a sustaining move dressed as a bold one: improving the existing product on the exact metric (taste) the existing customer base and the market already compete on, aimed at existing customers, to defend existing share. It is not disruptive — it doesn't create a new market, attack from below, or serve a non-consuming segment.

The incumbent being defended here *is the brief's own company* — Gate 1's framing ("defend core cola market share") is textbook incumbent-under-attack language, and the response chosen (match the attacker's taste profile via full replacement) concedes the competitor's terms of competition rather than contesting them on a different axis (distribution, price, ritual/nostalgia positioning — the very axis Gate 1 admits is untested).

Who loses market position if this "lands": the brief doesn't ask this question outward at all — it's entirely about defending against the external competitor named in Gate 1, with no analysis of whether the original-formula segment itself becomes contestable by a third mover once vacated.

**Strongest objection:** A defensive brief with no data on the dimension (identity/nostalgia) most likely to be the original's actual competitive moat is choosing to abandon the one asset it hasn't measured.

---

### Value Chain — The chokepoint being moved is the one thing not being tested

This capability sits at the product-formulation node of the value chain — furthest upstream, hardest to reverse, and the single highest-leverage point in the whole chain for margin effect: get it right and defend all downstream margin (bottling, distribution, retail shelf presence); get it wrong and the reversal cost cascades through every downstream relationship (bottler contracts, retail shelf-space negotiations, brand equity as a balance-sheet asset).

The margin effect is framed entirely as protective ("defend core cola market share") with no analysis of margin risk from the discontinuation itself — brand equity and shelf/negotiating leverage built on decades of the original formula's identity is a value-chain asset with no line item anywhere in this brief.

Control of the chokepoint (the recipe) is total and unilateral — no external party is consulted in Gate 3 — which is precisely why the untested [?] items (nostalgia attachment, parallel-launch alternative) carry outsized risk: a full-control chokepoint decision made on partial evidence has no natural check downstream to catch it before shelf.

**Strongest objection:** This is the highest-irreversibility node in the value chain, and it's the node getting the least incremental evidence relative to its blast radius — the brief spends its evidence budget on the reversible variable (formula taste, easily re-tested) and none on the irreversible one (discontinuation reaction, untestable after the fact once the original is gone).

---

> **Disruptive Innovation and Value Chain diverge from Jobs-to-Be-Done on urgency framing** — DI and Value Chain read this as a defensible, correctable sustaining move where the risk is mainly about *which* lever to pull (taste vs. distribution vs. positioning). JTBD reads the risk as categorical — the brief may be solving the wrong job entirely, in which case no amount of taste-formula tuning fixes it. This is a decision about whether the brief's core bet is *underspecified* or *misdirected*, and council does not resolve it.

> **Self-Determination Theory and Service Blueprint converge, but from different mechanisms, on the same missing evidence** — SDT names the autonomy cost of removing choice; Service Blueprint names the absent frontstage/communication design for that removal. They land on the same "what's missing" line in the brief for different reasons — one is about psychological ownership, the other about operational readiness. Worth keeping both frames when this brief gets addressed, since fixing one (adding a communication plan) does not fix the other (the choice is still gone).
