## Gate 1 — Problem
Consumers associate electric vehicles with compromise: slow, unattractive, limited range. Prior mass-market attempts were discontinued. [D] No car has proven EVs can be aspirational rather than a compromise. [A]

## Gate 2 — Right thing
Mission-level bet on transportation electrification. Sequencing strategy: sell a $100k low-volume sports car first (Roadster) to prove desirability and fund R&D toward a cheaper, higher-volume model later. [A] Uses repurposed laptop-style battery cells rather than purpose-built automotive cells. [A]

## Gate 3 — Built right
No dealer network — sells direct. [A] No existing automotive manufacturing scale; sourcing chassis/components from a low-volume specialist (Lotus). [D] Path from low-volume luxury to mass-market unproven — automotive is capital-intensive and littered with well-funded failures (DeLorean, Fisker's predecessors). [A]

## Failure modes
1. Capital intensity of auto manufacturing outpaces ability to reach the cheaper second model — company runs out of money before the mass-market pivot.
2. Battery cost curve doesn't fall fast enough to hit a price point mass-market buyers will pay. [?]
