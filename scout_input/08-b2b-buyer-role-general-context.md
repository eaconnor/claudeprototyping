# General B2B buying-role literature — economic/technical buyer overlap (not MSP-specific)

[CS: LOW — outside MSP-specific domain, general SaaS/B2B sales methodology sources (Sandler, Miller Heiman-style "buying roles" frameworks)]

Search across general B2B sales methodology sources (Sandler, Dock.us, assorted SaaS sales blogs) surfaced the standard "buying roles" model: Economic Buyer, Technical Buyer/Validator, User/Operator, Champion, Procurement — treated as a fixed five-role framework, most often illustrated with enterprise deal examples.

Relevant qualifier found across multiple sources, stated as general sales doctrine rather than researched finding:
> "A single person can hold multiple roles, or these roles can be dispersed among several individuals... In enterprise deals they are almost always a different person from your day-to-day champion."

The implication (not stated outright in any single source, but consistent across the genre): role consolidation — technical user and economic buyer being the same person — is understood in B2B sales methodology to be **more common in smaller organizations** and less common as company size and deal complexity increase. No source quantifies this for MSPs specifically, or for SMB software generally. This is sales-training folk wisdom, not survey data. [CS: LOW]

**Bearing on Beth's push-back:** MSPs skew small by every dataset found (Kaseya: 31% ≤10 employees, CompTIA/industry consensus: MSP is a small-business-dominated category). If the general B2B doctrine that "small org = role consolidation" holds, it would support Beth's read that the technician/economic-buyer split is overdrawn for a meaningful share of the MSP population — but this is an inference chain, not a finding. Flagging the inference explicitly so it isn't laundered into a fact.
