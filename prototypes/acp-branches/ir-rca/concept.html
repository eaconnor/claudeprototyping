Live IR/RCA — Root Cause, Written As the Incident Happens

A coworker that builds the customer-ready root-cause report while the incident is still open — cross-stack, confidence-tagged, defensible by morning. This is a sketch to react to. Nothing here is built.

01The problem

Post-incident, MSPs reconstruct what happened by hand, days later, from fragmented logs across six tools and three threads — often relying on one senior tech's memory.

The wicked bit: the report is a liability artifact. A wrong root cause, narrated confidently, destroys the MSP's credibility in one sentence — and no tool today distinguishes a telemetry-sourced fact from a confident guess.

02The wedge — during, not after

"Doing the RCA during the incident is equivalent to helping resolve it." — MSP, source interview

Post-hoc, the same report collapses into a smaller, commoditizable text-synthesizer. During the incident, it's part of the response — that's the wedge, and the reason a client pays for it.

03Value prop

For MSPs carrying incident-response obligations they can't staff with a full SOC, the IR/RCA coworker assembles a cross-stack, confidence-tagged root-cause report in real time. Unlike manual post-hoc RCA or a generic SIEM export, it produces two calibrated outputs — a technical evidence chain and a client/insurer narrative — with a human review gate before anything faces a client.

04Why now

Compliance-driven willingness to pay is real here: this is the #1 named driver on cyber-insurance policies. MSPs are already trying to prove containment to a client, an auditor, and an insurer — the gap is that the defensible version of that proof only exists days later, if at all.
