## Sketch 1 — The EBR Practice Companion
*(Package 3, re-framed confidence-first — the July twist made structural)*

- **The friction:** MSP operators are expected to run the business-strategy meeting that wins referrals, prevents churn, and manages liability — but they're technical people facing non-technical rooms. The prep costs hours they don't have `[CS: LOW — echo, directional]`, and the confidence isn't purchasable at all: the tools that exist generate reports, and nobody walks out of a report generator braver. Meanwhile the framework author's own magic-wand list was three parts confidence to one part time. `[CS: VERIFIED — 07-30]`

- **The design question:** How might we get a technical operator into a CFO's room feeling like a consultant — with fifteen minutes of prep on top of an account state that never went cold?

- **Why this solution:** For MSP operators who dread the quarterly room, the Practice Companion keeps the living documents warm between meetings, drafts the register in consequence-grammar (data → risk → what it means → what happens if not), grades it in red/yellow/green, scripts the talking points in two registers (CFO gets ROI, owner gets strategy), and rehearses the five hardest questions before the room does. Unlike report generators — including the one that already died at Zomentum — it transfers *confidence*, not just minutes, and its scaffolding fades as the operator's own notes take over (manual-first, her pedagogy).

- **N-able superpower:** Stefanie's craft, codified with her own participation — Lewis asked for "her as an agent," and she wrote the feature list herself unprompted `[CS: VERIFIED]`. First-party telemetry for every pull. The Head Nerd enablement channel (boot camps, office hours) as a distribution vector no competitor has.

- **Death squares:** Q2 unfielded — the operator has never been studied; confidence-transfer is evidenced only from the trainer's seat. The counsel gate — everything drafted into the register inherits the liability weight. W4 — if the scaffold doesn't fade, we automate away the reps that build the skill.

## Sketch 2 — The First-Meeting Aggregator
*(Stefanie's piece ii — pre-sales, proposed unprompted 07-30)*

- **The friction:** MSPs win or lose deals on the quality of the first meeting `[CS: VERIFIED — Stefanie, 07-30]`, but most have no marketing system and no research capacity — they walk in generic, "vanilla marketing," knowing nothing about the prospect's business, industry, or risks, and pitch technology to people who don't buy technology.

- **The design question:** How might an MSP walk into a first meeting already knowing the prospect's industry, risks, and world — without a research department?

- **Why this solution:** For MSPs pitching new business, the Aggregator assembles the prospect dossier before the meeting — industry trends ("agent-jacking in dental is up — are you seeing this?"), job postings, news, a compliance profile — and stages the readiness assessment that becomes the future risk register if the deal closes. Unlike generic pitch prep, it makes the MSP sound like they already get the client — "You get me. Why would I leave you for anybody talking generic?" — and unlike a point tool, its artifact *grows*: prospect dossier → assessment → living register. The funnel and the ritual share one memory.

- **N-able superpower:** the general-expertise layer — base-wide patterns, vertical intelligence, the translation grammar — that no single MSP can self-build and no competitor holds at our scale. And structural continuity: the same data spine serves pre-sales, onboarding, and every EBR after.

- **Death squares:** pre-sales tooling is a crowded, cheap category — the differentiator is the continuity into the register, which only pays off if Sketch 1 or 3 exists. Web-research agents are the most commoditized layer of the stack ("build it in Cloud in a day" risk). No demand evidence yet beyond the author's own proposal — n=1, enablement seat.

## Sketch 3 — The Business Visibility Layer
*(Beth's leap, 07-31 — the reframe the others might live inside)*

- **The friction:** MSPs are responsible for "basically all of it" — but knowing what's going on with each client's business is *episodic*: hand-assembled each quarter by two scarce people, cold in between. And the blindness runs both ways — "I never hear from my MSP" is the named churn trigger, because the client can't see the invisible work either. Both sides of the relationship fly blind between meetings, and the meeting is the only lens anyone owns.

- **The design question:** How might an MSP know what's going on with every client's business — easily, all the time — and let the client feel known in return?

- **Why this solution:** For MSPs managing dozens of client relationships, the Visibility Layer keeps living state per client — assets, risks, goals, industry context, the working documents Stefanie ran a hundred accounts on by hand — warm, continuously, from first-party telemetry plus the industry layer. The QBR becomes its quarterly render; Env Health its daily one; the first-meeting dossier its pre-sales render. Unlike meeting-prep tools, it doesn't compress the prep problem — it dissolves it: there is no cold start because nothing went cold.

- **N-able superpower:** it dissolves the corpus's own oldest divergence — QBR tops what buyers want lifted (drag-rank 2.90) while Env Health tops per-agent value (3.59); one layer, two views `[D — n=119]`. First-party full-stack telemetry is the only way to build it without permission from competitors. The ledger + runbook substrate is already built (49/49, 52/52) — renders are wiring, not construction.

- **Death squares:** "build it into the RMM" caps willingness-to-pay for visibility features — buyers expect them inside tooling they already own `[R]`. An always-on data layer makes the legal/DPA surface *bigger* than per-meeting pulls — Q1 gets heavier. Coworker-to-coworker orchestration is a v1 non-goal in the canonical spec — the layer's architecture fights the current build plan. And it's a platform bet wearing a feature's evidence.
