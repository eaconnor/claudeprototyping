# Ecoverse Kickoff — Beth's walk-in notes
*Brian Mackie's "Ecoverse Product & GTM Strategy" working draft · 3 Sept 2026*
*You've not read it. You don't need to. Everything you need is on this page.*

## 30-second orientation (read this in the lift)

It's a working-draft strategy for a **new endpoint-management product** — enter with smaller internal IT teams (under ~1,000 devices), then expand to MSPs. The pitch: "re-run the NinjaOne playbook, but AI-native."

It's a genuine first draft with good bones — **but it was partly written with Copilot, and it shows.** The polished slides read as if the strategy is settled. It isn't. The most important questions are sitting in Brian's own hand-written margin notes, not in the slides. Your job in the room is to move those margin notes to the centre of the table.

**Where we actually sit:** not open water. Two competitors are already doing the exact "simple, cheap, AI, under-1,000-devices" thing the deck calls greenfield — **Action1** (thousands of customers, free under 200 devices) and **Flamingo** ($1/device, open-source). Microsoft Intune sits underneath all of it, already free in the customer's Microsoft licence.

---

## If you say only one thing, say this

> "This is a strong start, but the slides are more finished than the thinking. The three questions that decide whether this works are in Brian's margin notes, not on the slides. Can we make those three the agenda today?"

That single move reframes the whole meeting and makes *you* the person who read it properly — without having read it.

---

## The three questions to force (this is the meeting)

**1. What actually makes someone leave NinjaOne?**
Brian asks this himself in a margin note — and the slides never answer it. Without an answer, the MSP half of the strategy has no engine.
> Ask: *"What's the one thing Ecoverse does that makes a happy Ninja customer switch? If we can't name it, we're not ready to commit to the MSP motion."*

**2. Is "greenfield" real, or is the seat already taken?**
The deck calls under-1,000-device IT "greenfield" — then, a few slides later, shows Action1 already winning that exact space with a free tier. Both can't be true. This is the word that drives the whole "IT Pro first" plan.
> Ask: *"If Action1 is already here and free, what's our answer to a free tier — not a longer feature list, an actual business-model answer?"*

**3. Does the AI do things *for* the technician, or *instead of* them?**
The deck says "super automated, AI executes" — but it also worries, in a margin note, about technicians fearing for their jobs. For the small, generalist IT teams we're targeting, that's the whole product decision, not a design detail.
> Ask: *"Does the AI act and tell them, or propose and let them approve? We're targeting small teams who need to stay in control — we should pick one on purpose."*

## Things to NOT let the room ratify

- **"Winning formula: simplicity / AI / openness / scale."** Sounds great, means nothing specific — it could describe any software company. Don't let it be treated as the strategy.
- **The tidy four-phase plan.** Too neat. Real plans are lumpier. It's a template, not yet a decision.
- **The capability tables in the back.** The "status" columns are *blank*. We don't yet know what's built. Nobody should leave thinking delivery is understood.
- **The strategy being treated as "signed off."** It's a personal working draft from a Copilot folder — it's a conversation starter, not an approved plan. Say that kindly, but say it.

## Numbers — what's safe to quote, what isn't

**Safe to say out loud (with "roughly" / "reportedly"):**
- Action1 is reportedly around **$12–13M revenue, growing ~76% a year**, free under 200 devices. → proof the space is *live*, not empty.
- Flamingo launched late 2025 at **$1/device**, open-source. → proof price pressure is coming from below.

**Do NOT quote as fact in the room:**
- Any of the market-size / MSP-segment percentages (the Canalys / Gartner figures). The deck itself says they're reused internally, "likely high," and must be checked before use. If they come up: *"Let's confirm those before anyone builds a plan on them."*
- Our own internal ICP numbers — the source slides aren't attached, so we can't stand behind the exact figures yet.

## Our real strength (make sure it gets said — it's buried)

The one thing competitors **can't copy**: our **endpoint agent and the data underneath it** — plus our existing install base and brand. Brian has it as a side-note ("our agent secret sauce"). It should be front and centre.
> Say: *"Our edge isn't being cheaper or simpler — Action1 and Flamingo already own that. It's the agent, the data, and the trust we already have. That's what we should build the story on."*

## If you get put on the spot

- **"Do you like it?"** → *"I like that it's honest about what it doesn't know yet. The bones are good. It's not a plan we can commit to until we answer the three open questions — and those are answerable."*
- **"Should we build this?"** → *"The problem is real and someone's already making money in it, so yes it's worth pursuing. Whether we win depends entirely on having an answer to a free competitor. Let's not confuse 'the market is real' with 'we've decided how to win.'"*
- **"Is it AI-generated?"** → *"Partly, and that's fine — the AI did the scaffolding, Brian did the thinking. You can see it in his margin notes. The risk is only if we mistake the polished scaffolding for the strategy."*
- **"What would you change first?"** → *"Put the three margin-note questions on a decision slide and make today about answering them."*
