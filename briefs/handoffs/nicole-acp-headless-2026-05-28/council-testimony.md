## Charter cluster

### CHTR · Moore positioning statement / STRAT

**Sign** — The brief's problem statement reads: *"MSPs spend enormous time on repeatable operational tasks across multiple product surfaces."* No Moore-style positioning sentence is drafted: *"For [target MSP] who [unmet need], ACP Headless is a [category] that [key benefit] unlike [alternative]."* The phrase "unlike [alternative]" is absent.

**Hypothesis** — The brief has an Initiative but no positioning. The team can describe what ACP Headless *is* but cannot complete the sentence *unlike what*. This usually means the alternative is "current behavior" (status quo) — which Moore's research says is the hardest competitor to displace.

**Kill criterion** — If Nicole drafts the positioning sentence and "unlike [alternative]" parses cleanly to a named competitor or named status-quo behavior, the Charter is real. If she cannot fill it without bluffing, the Charter is a wish.

**Force level** — Must.

**Evidence** — [CS: VERIFIED] for positioning sentence absent (grep of brief). [CS: MEDIUM] for the Moore claim about status quo as hardest competitor — well-sourced in *Crossing the Chasm*, not personally verified for MSP segment.

**Cannot see** — CHTR reads documents, not rooms. CHTR cannot see whether the team would agree on the positioning if drafted. The Charter exists *operationally* (Nicole wrote a brief) but may not exist *strategically* (we have not loaded n-able's FY27 portfolio strategy to know what force level Headless sits at).

## Sign-reading cluster

### SIGN · Peirce + Tavory/Timmermans / ABD

**Sign** — One sourced claim about MSP behavior: Coworker doc states *"MSPs spend enormous time on repeatable operational tasks."* That is one sign. No interview data, no support-ticket pattern, no NN/g study, no field observation. The brief repeats the headless thesis as if repetition were corroboration.

**Hypothesis** — Single-sign confirmation pattern. Peirce: abduction is inference to the *best* explanation, but "best" requires alternative hypotheses tested against the sign. Tavory/Timmermans: alternative casing must be surfaced. Alternatives not considered: (a) the time is *the job* not the friction; (b) the tooling is fine and the problem is alert quality; (c) MSPs *want* to be in the products because that is where their craft lives.

**Kill criterion** — Run 5 MSP interviews. If ≥3 of 5 say "yes, the tool-switching is the worst part," the sign is corroborated. If <3, the sign is a single-source artifact.

**Force level** — Must.

**Evidence** — [CS: HIGH] that single-source claims are inadequate for design decisions (Tavory/Timmermans methodological standard). [CS: LOW] that the alternatives listed above are actually present — they are abductively plausible, not field-observed.

**Cannot see** — SIGN cannot tell whether the MSP would *prefer* a fragmented surface that lets them sense state visually, even if it costs time. SIGN observes signals but does not measure preference.

## Position / adoption cluster

**Sign** — The brief proposes that MSPs operate without opening UEM/Cove/Adlumin — a deliberately "worse" experience on traditional product-UI metrics (feature depth, configurability, visual richness) but "better" on a new dimension (time-to-resolution for repeatable tasks). ## Jobs cluster

### JOBS · JTBD functional + emotional/social / INNOV

**Sign** — The brief's job description: "reduce repeatable operational work." This is a functional job statement. No emotional or social job is named. The Coworker doc, however, hints at emotional ground: *"MSPs want to be the expert their customer trusts."*

**Hypothesis** — Three jobs are active, not one. **Functional:** reduce time on ops. **Emotional:** feel in control when incidents escalate. **Social:** look competent to the MSP's customer. The headless model serves the functional job but may *undermine* the emotional and social jobs — MSPs cannot perform competence through visible product mastery if they do not open the products.

**Kill criterion** — In the 5-MSP walkthrough, ask "what do you do when your customer asks how you fixed something?" If MSPs reference the product UI in their answer, the social job depends on product visibility. If they reference the Value Ledger, the social job has been migrated.

**Force level** — Must.

**Evidence** — [CS: HIGH] on JTBD's three-job framework (Christensen / Ulwick). [CS: LOW] on the specific three jobs for MSPs — abductive, not interviewed.

**Cannot see** — JOBS cannot tell whether removing visible products *helps* the MSP because product UIs are the cognitive load source, not the competence performance vehicle. JOBS reads what is being hired; it does not adjudicate whether the hire is healthy.

## Needs cluster

### NEEDS · Self-Determination Theory / PSYCH

**Sign** — Trust ladder (L0–L4) ratchets up. No demotion path in the brief or prototype. Coworker promotion is one-way.

**Hypothesis** — SDT's three needs (autonomy · competence · relatedness) are at risk asymmetrically:
- **Autonomy** — supported by trust ladder *if* MSP can demote. Currently cannot. Autonomy is unilateral.
- **Competence** — erodes over three-year horizon if MSP only ever approves Coworker output. Skill atrophy is a documented risk in agent-mediated work (NN/g 2023+).
- **Relatedness** — MSP's relationship to their customer routes through the Value Ledger, not through visible product mastery. Whether that supports or erodes relatedness is unknown.

**Kill criterion** — Design a demotion path (any MSP can move any Coworker down a trust level for any reason, with audit trail). If the design lands cleanly, the autonomy objection retires. If it surfaces edge-case complexity (does demotion roll back in-flight work?), the autonomy claim is fragile.

**Force level** — Must.

**Evidence** — [CS: HIGH] on SDT (Deci/Ryan, decades of research). [CS: MEDIUM] on agent-mediated skill atrophy — NN/g and Anthropic-internal preliminary studies, not a settled finding.

**Cannot see** — NEEDS cannot tell whether MSPs actively *want* deskilling — outsourcing the cognitive load might feel like a gift, not a loss. SDT names the needs; it does not survey whether users feel them as binding constraints.

## Landscape cluster

### LAND · Wardley Mapping / STRAT

**Sign** — The Value Ledger is positioned as a customer-facing artifact (per Control Plane doc). On a Wardley map, customer-visible value evolution moves from custom-built → product → commodity → utility. Trust signals for AI agents are currently in the *custom-built* to *product* transition; the brief positions them as if already at *product*.

**Hypothesis** — The trust ledger as designed is a custom-built artifact ("our trust math") in a market that has not commoditized. Whoever lands at the *product* phase first gets to define the category — which is the Value Chain chokepoint already named by Council. But the brief skips the work of moving the artifact through evolution; it assumes the artifact arrives mature.

**Kill criterion** — Map three competitive trust-signal approaches on a Wardley evolution axis. If ACP's approach is genuinely ahead, the chokepoint thesis holds. If two competitors are at the same evolution stage, the moat is timing, not architecture.

**Force level** — Should.

**Evidence** — [CS: HIGH] on Wardley's evolution axis methodology. [CS: UNKNOWN] on where competitors sit — no competitive scan in the brief.

**Cannot see** — LAND cannot tell whether MSPs care about which evolution stage the trust signal sits at. Wardley reads supplier dynamics, not user perception.

## Economics cluster

### ECON · Economic buyer / MKT

**Sign** — The brief names no economic buyer for ACP Headless. ACP is described as bundled with existing N-able subscriptions. No per-Coworker SKU, no per-execution pricing, no margin-share with MSPs. The Doblin Profit Model dimension scored 0/2.

**Hypothesis** — Without a named buying motion, ACP Headless is a feature of the existing subscription, not a product. Sales does not have a line item to sell. Customer Success does not have an expansion path. The defensibility weakness Doblin flagged maps directly to a revenue model weakness: there is no transaction that captures the value Headless creates.

**Kill criterion** — Name the buying motion. Three candidates: (a) included in current bundle, sold as a *retention* lever; (b) per-Coworker pricing, sold as an *expansion* lever; (c) value-share on MSP margin saved, sold as a *partnership* lever. Pick one. If none can be picked, the brief has no theory of value capture.

**Force level** — Must (before Tier 2).

**Evidence** — [CS: VERIFIED] that the brief names no economic buyer (grep). [CS: HIGH] on Economic Buyer methodology (Miller Heiman strategic selling, decades of B2B practice).

**Cannot see** — ECON cannot tell whether MSP buying psychology will accept any of the three buying motions. Economic Buyer reads the value-capture mechanism; it does not predict adoption.

# Cross-Talk

*Voices using each other's data. Three required moves. New claims that no single voice produced alone.*

## Cross-Talk Move 1 · Direct Response

**JOBS reads SIGN's data, offers different hypothesis.**

SIGN observes: *"MSPs spend enormous time on repeatable operational tasks"* (single-source, Coworker doc). SIGN's hypothesis: not enough alternative casing — the time might be the job, not the friction.

JOBS reads the same sign and goes further: *the time spent in product UIs is the MSP's emotional and social job*. MSPs are not just executing tasks in those UIs — they are performing competence in front of their customer. If the prototype removes the products, it does not remove a friction; it removes the stage on which the MSP's craft is visible.

**New claim, surfaced only by cross-talk:** The headless thesis is not "remove the products." It is "migrate the MSP's competence performance from the product UI to the ACP." That is a much harder design problem than the brief acknowledges — and it makes the Value Ledger the central artifact, because the Ledger is the new stage.

## Cross-Talk Move 2 · Blind-Spot Supply

**CHTR admitted it cannot see whether the team believes the Charter.** META supplies the answer using Owen Originator/Contributors lens.

CHTR's blind spot: a positioning statement that parses cleanly is necessary but not sufficient. Organizational consent is invisible to the document.

META reads the brief's authorship: **1 named Originator (Nicole), 0 named Contributors.** A multi-type Doblin expansion (Profit Model, Channel, Brand, Network) would require a Contributors panel including Sales, Marketing, Eng leadership, and at minimum one MSP design partner. None are named. The Charter exists on paper. Organizational consent is structurally absent.

**New claim, surfaced only by cross-talk:** Before Tier 2 ships, Nicole must name the Contributors panel for each Doblin dimension Council recommended (Profit Model → Sales lead; Channel → Marketing lead; Brand → Marketing+Product; Network → Eng+Community). If she cannot name them by 2026-06-19, the brief is a single-author wish, regardless of how strong the prototype reads.

## Cross-Talk Move 3 · Productive Tension

**JOBS-functional vs. NEEDS-SDT-competence on the same brief text.**

JOBS reads: *"MSPs hiring to reduce repeatable operational work."* That is the functional hire. The brief serves it.

NEEDS-SDT reads the same brief and the same trust ladder, and finds: *MSPs who only ever approve Coworker output will atrophy on competence within three years*. The brief's success scenario (full L3 adoption across all Coworkers) is the worst case for the MSP's competence trajectory.

**Both readings are sourced.** JTBD is the canonical innovation framework Nicole has run on previous briefs. SDT is the canonical psychological needs framework, with multi-decade research. They are not opponents — they are reading at different time horizons. JOBS reads the next quarter. SDT reads the next three years.

**The productive tension is the design decision Nicole owns:** Does the trust ladder ratchet only up (serves JOBS, harms NEEDS) or does it allow demotion plus *competence preservation rituals* (a periodic mode where the MSP must do the work the Coworker normally does, to maintain the skill)? The second option serves both jobs at the cost of design complexity.

**This is not a research gap.** It is a deliberate product choice between "MSPs as approvers" and "MSPs as preserved experts." Tier 2 cannot ship without naming which one.
